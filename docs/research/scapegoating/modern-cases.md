# Scapegoating Cases: Screening Notes

**Prepared:** 18 October 2026, by Claude (Anthropic) at the author's request, from two delegated web searches  
**Status:** incomplete; the Post Office Horizon and Boeing 737 MAX vettings stopped before they reported; no manuscript changes made

The search asked for a modern, well-documented case in which blaming a person or unit severely damaged an organization in a way gradual change would not have. The original screen rated Mitsubishi Fuso's wheel hubs a pass on all four tests. The subsequent review of the cited Supreme Court judgment supports a narrower finding: an inadequate attribution to users coexisted with a failure to recall that the court connected to a later fatal accident. The judgment does not establish the proposed route to corporate financial loss. No all-four pass is established here. Wells Fargo, the one case vetted in depth in the original pass, passes that pass's scapegoat test but no official finding inspected here attributes its organizational losses specifically to blaming.

## Tests

| Test | A case passes when |
| --- | --- |
| T1 Scapegoat | An independent body (a court, statutory inquiry, accident investigator, regulator or independent board investigation) has established that blame was placed on a person or unit inside the organization, or in its operating chain, out of proportion to their causal contribution |
| T2 Mechanism | The organization's severe damage came from the act of blaming (prosecuting, firing, concealing the real cause behind the blame), not merely from the underlying defect or misconduct |
| T3 Step, not drift | The damage arrived as a discrete step (a judgment, a second accident, a collapse, a forced exit) that gradual fixing of the real cause would not have produced |
| T4 Record | Modern (roughly 1990 onward) and documented by primary records |

The Wells Fargo vetting was set T1–T3 only.

These are screening conditions, not interchangeable outcomes. T2 must distinguish harm to people from loss to the organization and identify which causal link a source establishes. A discrete event alone cannot establish T3's counterfactual about gradual repair. In the inherited screen, “fail” sometimes records missing evidence or a scope exclusion rather than evidence that scapegoating did not occur; citizens in the childcare-benefits and Robodebt cases fall outside this screen's internal-chain condition only.

## Wells Fargo (vetted)

### The scapegoat and the finding that established it

The Independent Directors' report of 10 April 2017 [P1] found the root cause in the Community Bank's distorted sales culture and performance management. A decentralized structure and leaders who played the problem down (Tolstedt) let it run, and Stumpf was too slow to act. Leaders blamed individual wrongdoers: “its solution generally was to terminate the offending employee,” without looking at causes or managers (pp. 5–6). About 5,300 were fired between 1 January 2011 and 7 March 2016 (p. 16).

The DOJ Statement of Facts, which the bank admitted [P11], says more than 23,000 employees were referred and more than 5,300 fired in 2011–2016, almost all at branch level (¶¶30–31). Leaders led gatekeepers to see “individual misconduct rather than the sales model itself” (¶28). The Comptroller, after a 38-day hearing before an administrative law judge [P13], found the misconduct “systemic, as it resulted from the Bank's business model.”

Caveat: these findings show that the cause was misattributed and the punishment lopsided. They do not show that the fired staff were innocent.

### Damage steps

- 8 Sep 2016: CFPB $100M, OCC $35M, Los Angeles City Attorney $50M; about 2 million accounts [P3].
- 20 Sep 2016: Stumpf told the Senate the 5,300 were 1% of staff and accepted full responsibility. Warren called it gutless and urged him to resign. Menendez: “this is not the work of 5,300 bad apples” [P4]. Market value fell more than $25B by late September [S1].
- 12 Oct 2016: Stumpf leaves, effective immediately [P5]. In October, new checking accounts fell 44% and card applications 50% [S2].
- April 2017: Tolstedt terminated for cause; clawbacks of about $69M from Stumpf and $67M from Tolstedt [P2].
- 2 Feb 2018: asset cap at the end-2017 size, about $1.95T [P6, S6]. Shares fell 9% [S3].
- 2018: $1B to the CFPB and OCC over auto insurance and mortgage rate-locks [P8]; $575M to the states [P9].
- 21 Feb 2020: $3B to the DOJ and SEC [P11].
- The cap's cost, per Bloomberg: about $4B by 2020 [S4]; “more than $36 billion in profits” by 2025, method not visible [S5]. The agent found no academic dollar estimate and no authoritative penalty total. The penalties listed here add up to about $4.76B (the agent's sum).

### From blaming to damage, and the strongest counterargument

Official findings:

- Firing people left the cause in place, fixes were piecemeal, and the problem kept growing. The board learned of the 5,300 only through the settlements [P1].
- Sales targets rose nearly every year through 2013 [P11], and misconduct did not fall in 2013–2016 [P13].
- Tolstedt admitted hiding termination data from examiners. Only “the most egregious .01 to .05 percent” of flagged staff were investigated [P12].
- The Fed cited “widespread consumer abuses and other compliance breakdowns” and governance [P6]. Only its letter to Stumpf deals with the response: he “continued to support the sales goals that were a major cause” and protected executives [P7].

No official body says the damage came from the blaming.

Counterargument: the damage followed the size of the misconduct, its concealment, and other abuses (the Fed's letter to the board names auto insurance [P7]). Disclosure was already under way: Los Angeles sued in May 2015 [S8]. And the bank fired too few for misconduct, not too many. The OCC alleged that leaders limited misconduct firings and paused monitoring (¶¶103, 213). Meanwhile about 8,520 were fired for sales performance, including missed goals (¶75), which is the sales model itself, not the blame response [P10].

### Status as of October 2026

- The cap was lifted on 3 June 2025, with all conditions met [P14]. The whole 2018 order ended on 5 March 2026 [P15].
- Stumpf: OCC $17.5M penalty and industry ban (2020) [P10].
- Tolstedt: OCC $17M and ban; guilty plea to obstructing a bank examination [P12]. In September 2023 she received three years' probation, six months' home confinement and a $100,000 fine. The judge said it would be unacceptable to have “one defendant bear the weight of this entire scandal” [S7].
- Russ Anderson: $10M and ban. The agent could not confirm the status of her appeal after April 2025 [P13, S9].

### Verdict

1. T1 pass: the board's committee and the DOJ facts the bank admitted put the firings on branch staff and the cause in leadership's sales model; the OCC decision after a full hearing agrees.
2. T2 partial: the findings tie the firing-first approach to the misconduct continuing, but not to the damage; the asset cap rests on the abuses and on governance.
3. T3 partial: the damage came in discrete steps, but disclosure was already under way before the blaming, and the way out of the cap was gradual repair; the counterfactual is unproven.

## Screened cases

**BP Texas City, 2005.** BP's May 2005 interim report blamed the isomerization-unit supervisors and operators, with discipline up to termination ([CNN](https://money.cnn.com/2005/05/18/news/international/bp_refinery.dj/)). The CSB (2007) found deficiencies “at all levels of the BP Corporation”, and the Baker Panel found material deficiencies across BP's US refineries ([CSB](https://www.csb.gov/u-s-chemical-safety-board-concludes-organizational-and-safety-deficiencies-at-all-levels-of-the-bp-corporation-caused-march-2005-texas-city-disaster-that-killed-15-injured-180/)). Later links: the CSB chair told Congress the 2006 Prudhoe Bay spill showed striking similarities ([OHS](https://ohsonline.com/articles/2007/06/csb-chairman-finds-striking-similarities-in-two-bp-accidents.aspx)). OSHA's record $87.4M penalty (2009) included 270 failure-to-abate notices ([ISHN](https://www.ishn.com/articles/88857-osha-fines-bp-a-record-87m-for-failing-to-fix-refinery-hazards-11-2)). The agent found no National Commission text tying Macondo to Texas City. The damage came from the explosion and the unfixed hazards, not from the blaming. T1 pass, T2 fail, T3 partial (the steps came from not fixing), T4 pass.

**Dutch childcare benefits (toeslagenaffaire).** The Tax Administration wrongly branded parents as deliberate fraudsters. The parliamentary inquiry's report *Ongekend onrecht* (17 Dec 2020) found that rule-of-law principles were breached. It did not assess junior staff ([report](https://www.houseofrepresentatives.nl/sites/default/files/atoms/files/verslag_pok_definitief-en-gb.docx.pdf)). The cabinet resigned over it on 15 January 2021 ([AP](https://chicago.suntimes.com/2021/1/15/22233157/dutch-government-quits-over-welfare-scandal)). T1 fail (citizens), T2 pass, T3 pass, T4 pass.

**Olympus, 2011.** The board fired Woodford on 14 Oct 2011, claiming he had not grasped its management style and Japanese culture ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2012/05/29/249116.htm)). A panel led by a former Supreme Court judge found $1.7bn of hidden losses and management “rotten to the core”, and cited his treatment. Shares fell by up to 75% ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2011/12/06/business/olympus-scandal)). The firing is what exposed the fraud. T1 partial (he was a whistleblower, not wrongly blamed for a defect), T2 pass, T3 pass, T4 pass.

**Bawa-Garba and the GMC, 2015–18.** The GMC appealed her tribunal suspension and won erasure in 2018. The Court of Appeal restored the suspension, saying the tribunal could weigh the hospital's systemic failings ([judiciary](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/bawa-garba-media-statement-final.pdf)). The Williams review (June 2018) recommended removing the GMC's appeal power and keeping reflective material out of its evidence powers ([Capsticks](https://capsticks.com/insights/williams-rapid-policy-review-into-gross-negligence-manslaughter-makes-10-recommendations)). It linked the power to doctors' mistrust of the GMC ([MPS](https://www.medicalprotection.org/uk/articles/uk-healthcare-leaders-unite-in-call-for-gmc-to-be-finally-stripped-of-power-to-appeal-fitness-to-practice-decisions)). An unofficial Pulse poll found 52% of GPs had changed or stopped their written reflections ([Pulse](https://www.pulsetoday.co.uk/news/regulation/over-half-of-gps-change-or-stop-reflections-following-bawa-garba-case/)). The power still stands. In July 2026 the government agreed to consult again after BMA legal action ([BMA](https://www.bma.org.uk/bma-media-centre/bma-secures-government-climbdown-over-misleading-attempt-to-retain-gmc-s-right-to-appeal-tribunal-decisions)). T1 partial (her conviction stands), T2 pass, T3 partial (the loss of the power is still pending), T4 pass.

**Uber self-driving fatality, Tempe, 2018.** Only the safety driver, Vasquez, was charged. She pleaded guilty to endangerment in 2023 ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/07/28/1190866476)). The NTSB made her distraction the probable cause. It also cited Uber ATG's risk assessment, its oversight of operators and its controls on complacency, all stemming from “inadequate safety culture” ([NTSB](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/HWY18MH010.aspx)). The agent found nothing tying Uber's 2020 sale of ATG ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2020/12/07/944004278/after-once-touting-self-driving-cars-uber-sells-unit-to-refocus-on-core-business)) to her prosecution. T1 partial, T2 fail, T3 partial (the step was the crash), T4 pass.

**Robodebt.** Welfare recipients were issued unlawful debts. The Royal Commission (July 2023) called the scheme “crude and cruel” and made sealed referrals ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2023/07/australia-robodebt-royal-commission-hands-down-findings-recommends-referrals-for-prosecution/)). Costs exceed A$2.4bn, including a $548.5M settlement approved in 2026 ([AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/australia-s-biggest-class-action-settlement-approved)). T1 fail (citizens), T2 pass, T3 pass, T4 pass.

**Überlingen mid-air collision, 2002.** The controller on duty alone, Peter Nielsen, was murdered in 2004 by a man who lost his family in the crash ([swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/banking-fintech/skyguide-failures-blamed-for-plane-crash/5891884)). Some defendants blamed him. The Bülach court (2007) convicted four Skyguide staff and said two controllers on duty would have prevented the collision ([swissinfo](https://swissinfo.ch/eng/four-skyguide-employees-found-guilty/6089426)). No finding says Skyguide made him the scapegoat. T1 fail, T2 fail, T3 partial (the step was the collision), T4 pass.

**Toyota unintended acceleration, 2009–14.** Blame fell on floor mats and drivers. NHTSA–NASA (2011) found no electronic cause and put many complaints down to pedal misapplication ([MPR](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2011/02/08/toyota-investigation)). The $1.2bn deferred prosecution agreement (2014) punished Toyota for claiming the floor-mat recall fixed the root cause while the sticky-pedal problem remained ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-criminal-charge-against-toyota-motor-corporation-and-deferred)). T1 fail, T2 partial (punished for concealment, not scapegoating), T3 pass, T4 pass.

**GM ignition switch, 2014.** The Valukas report, commissioned by GM itself, led to 15 firings, more than half of them senior staff, including DeGiorgio. It cleared Barra. Senator Blumenthal said it let upper management off ([HR Reporter](https://www.hrreporter.com/focus-areas/safety/gm-top-executives-spared-in-internal-report-on-safety-failure/285329)). The $900M deferred prosecution agreement (2015) charged no individuals ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2015/09/gm-reaches-900-million-settlement-of-criminal-charges-over-faulty-ignition-switches/)). No independent body found the blame on individuals disproportionate. T1 fail, T2 fail, T3 partial, T4 pass.

## Candidates found by the screen

**Mitsubishi Fuso wheel hubs, 1999–2004.** After a bus wheel hub failed in 1999, quality staff told the ministry no action was needed. They relied on an in-house theory that blamed wear from users' poor maintenance. Japan's Supreme Court (2012, one judge dissenting) rejected that theory. It held that a pedestrian's death in Yokohama in 2002 was the realised risk of failing to recall ([court](https://www.courts.go.jp/english/Judgments/search/1140/index.html)).

- In March 2004 Fuso admitted the design flaw, recalled about 112,000 vehicles and linked 57 accidents since 1992 to the hubs ([Japan Times](https://info.japantimes.co.jp/weekly/news/nn2004/nn20040403a5.htm)).
- Seven former executives were arrested over the report that blamed maintenance ([Japan Times](https://info.japantimes.co.jp/weekly/news/nn2004/nn20040515a5.htm)).
- Daimler refused further funding and CEO Eckrodt quit, though that report mentions recalls only in general ([Japan Times](https://info.japantimes.co.jp/weekly/news/nn2004/nn20040501a4.htm)).
- Car sales in May 2004 fell nearly 60% ([AP](https://www2020.theautochannel.com/news/2004/06/02/198131.html)).

**Primary-record review, 18 October 2026.** The majority's reasoning in section 3(1) rejects an explanation confined to the user's maintenance and operating conditions as insufficient to dismiss a design-strength problem. Sections 2 and 3 address the defendants' duty, foreseeability and the avoidability of the fatal accident through recall. That supports a mechanism of misplaced attribution accompanying continued exposure to danger. It does not establish that a particular user was wholly without fault, identify a disproportionate personal sanction, or trace the later corporate losses to the blame response. Corporate-loss claims above still rely on the original secondary-source screen and involve other recalls and the earlier cover-up scandal. Revised T1, T2 and T3: partial; T4: pass for the judgment's bounded finding.

**News of the World, 2006–11.** A Commons committee (2010) criticised News International, the police and the PCC for leaving Goodman “as the sole scapegoat” ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/?p=45090)). Its 2012 report found that executives had misled it ([Inforrm](https://inforrm.org/2012/05/01/new-culture-media-and-sport-committee-reports-on-news-international-and-phone-hacking-laura-sandwell/amp/)). The paper closed days after the Milly Dowler revelation ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2011/7/10/scandal-hit-uk-newspaper-shuts-down)), and the BSkyB bid was dropped on 13 July 2011 ([Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2011/07/13/news-corp-withdraws-bskyb-bid)). T1 pass, T2 partial (the trigger was the hacking itself), T3 pass, T4 pass.

**JR West, Fukuchiyama Line, 2005.** JR West punished minor driver errors with retraining sessions. The accident commission (2007) found that the driver had just overrun a station. When he braked late he was probably preoccupied with excuses or with fear of punishment. The commission faulted the retraining programme ([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2007/06/29/national/jr-train-driver-faulted-in-final-report-on-crash/)). 107 people died and the company president resigned ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagasaki_derailment), a secondary source). T1 partial (a culture of blame rather than one scapegoat), T2 pass, T3 pass, T4 pass.

The screening agent originally ranked them:

1. Mitsubishi Fuso hubs: originally rated the only all-four pass. The primary-record review above narrows that assessment; a causal finding about the accident does not establish the corporate-loss claim.
2. JR West: the official investigator traced the disaster to the blaming itself.
3. News of the World: the clearest official finding that someone was made a scapegoat, plus a paper shut within days; weaker on mechanism.
