# Power and Accusation

**Provisional discussion text — 18 October 2026**

Prepared by Codex at the author's request, against public source `3529a77`. This proposes an argument for author review; it is not a statement that the author has adopted every premise. It is not included in the bound book. The proposed separation of the Tarot remains undecided.

The essay is followed by objections and author decisions. Source keys distinguish historical reports from the normative proposals and contemporary hypotheses developed here.

## The accusation must still be proved

The comparison with witch hunting fails if it makes criticism of AI into persecution by definition. A complaint about stolen work, a dangerous deployment, an abusive household, or a fraudulent product may be well founded. A person subjected to an unjust accusation may also have harmed someone in another way. Protection against the first does not discharge responsibility for the second.

Nor does a critic's inconsistency establish permission. Someone who condemns one exploitative practice while tolerating another may need to abandon both. The missing argument is whether the practices differ in a morally relevant respect, and what follows when they do not. An accusation of hypocrisy cannot supply the answer on its own.

The question examined here is how power over a technology and vulnerability to accusation can come apart. Who can determine its purposes, demand its use, retain its gains, and change its conditions? Who becomes identifiable when something goes wrong? Those questions concern different relations. Their separation makes scapegoating possible; evidence of that separation does not establish that it has occurred in every case.

## Agrippa and the limits of a defense

In their reconstruction of the Woippy proceeding of 1519, Vera Hoorens and Hans Renders describe Agrippa contesting the inquisitor's jurisdiction, the conduct of the proceeding, and an inference from the accused woman's mother to the woman herself. His theological argument appealed to baptism against a supposedly inherited demonic dedication. The reconstruction also reports his disparagement of witnesses on grounds that included being women. This account comes through Agrippa's correspondence, read here through the historians, rather than an independently inspected court record. [A1]

A defense can interrupt a persecution while retaining another exclusion. Rejecting an inherited charge does not by itself give women equal standing to testify. Saving this defendant does not establish a general opposition to witch trials. Neither limitation turns the intervention into nothing. It identifies what the defense accomplished and what it leaves untouched.

The modern obligation proposed here goes further than that defense. Protection against an unsupported charge should not depend on obtaining the right patron, performing the right allegiance, or proving membership in an approved community. The person accused must be able to contest the grounds of the accusation without first becoming acceptable to the accuser. This is a commitment argued for in the present, not a doctrine attributed to Agrippa.

## Work near misfortune

Philippa Carter's study of Richard Napier's English casebooks connects vulnerability to witchcraft suspicion with gendered work. Healthcare, childcare, food preparation, dairying, and animal care put women near sickness, death, and spoilage. Precarious workers could combine several such activities and their accompanying risks. Carter studies the formation of suspicion; she does not trace those reports into convictions or executions. Napier, the medical practitioner and astrologer receiving them, occupied a different position in the production of knowledge about suspected witchcraft. [C1]

The useful comparison is a relation between work and credibility. Being close enough to notice or manage a failure can make someone available to blame. Expertise can help a person act, but authority to explain what happened may belong elsewhere. My interpretation of the contrast with Napier concerns that unequal standing; it does not establish immunity for learned magicians.

A feminist account should follow the allocation of tasks, credentials, property, care, and credibility. It should examine who must remain available when ordinary processes fail, whose explanation is heard, and who can survive a damaged reputation. These arrangements need investigation in each setting. They cannot be deduced from the presence of a woman among the accused, or a man among those exercising authority.

A broad comparison between learned practitioners and persecuted outsiders requires evidence across places and periods. Napier and Metz cannot establish how often either group escaped prosecution. They support a narrower inquiry: how did particular institutions distinguish authorized knowledge from culpable association, and what resources allowed someone to contest that distinction?

## The household and the laboratory

Jo Freeman's account of informal power begins inside the women's liberation movement. Friendship networks can control information and decisions even where a group refuses formal offices. Participation through those networks also consumes time, disadvantaging people with work or other commitments. Freeman proposes explicit, distributed, and accountable authority; she does not claim that making rules explicit abolishes informal influence. [F1]

That argument gives a way to investigate a laboratory, collective, or shared household without allowing its declared ideals to settle the inquiry. Determine who controls housing, money, introductions, access to equipment, and the interpretation of disputes. Ask whether the person receiving a complaint depends on the person complained about. Ask what a member would lose by disagreeing while staying, as well as by leaving.

A shared house can provide care, affordable shelter, and sustained intellectual company. Concentrating those goods in one circle can also raise the cost of dissent. The question is whether a dispute over work or belief can jeopardize a person's home, income, and social life together. That is a proposed test of dependence, not a finding about every shared house or Rationalist community.

A group's description of itself as rational, egalitarian, sexually liberated, or devoted to humanity cannot resolve those questions. Consent to one relationship does not establish consent to every dependency attached to it. When a disagreement can jeopardize housing as well as friendship, a member needs protections that survive the disagreement. Those protections must reach people whose competence, desirability, or standing the inner circle disputes.

A feminist criticism must also examine the distribution of work required to sustain the supposedly independent thinker. Who maintains the room in which the brilliant argument happens? Who accommodates the emergency, absorbs the distress, and returns to unfinished work afterward? Treating care as an inexhaustible personal virtue can conceal decisions about time and resources. Changing who occupies the most prestigious office does not, by itself, change those decisions.

Equality on these terms requires more than permission to attend a discussion. Care and maintenance need time, resources, and an acknowledged place in decisions about the work. Affected workers need effective authority over deployment and its conditions; consultation alone can leave the power to impose risk intact. Members need ways to challenge decisions without cultivating a patron first. These are proposed institutional commitments. Their implementation would require choices about ownership, bargaining, and public provision that a reporting procedure cannot settle.

## Responsibility beyond the nearest person

Madeleine Clare Elish names a *moral crumple zone*: responsibility can be assigned to a human actor who had limited control over an automated system. The concept identifies a possible misallocation of responsibility, rather than proving that every operator blamed after a failure was powerless or blameless. [E1]

There is a documented organizational example outside AI. The Wells Fargo statement of facts attached to its 2020 deferred prosecution agreement records leadership assurances that directed attention toward individual misconduct rather than the sales model. It also records that almost all the relevant terminations and resignations were at branch level. The same record describes unauthorized and fraudulent accounts. It supports criticism of the allocation of responsibility without establishing the innocence of every employee involved. [W1]

This distinction matters. An employee can commit a wrongful act under a system whose designers and managers also bear responsibility. Establishing the act does not finish the investigation of its conditions. Discovering the conditions does not make the act disappear.

For a proposed AI deployment, follow the decisions before selecting a culprit. Who chose the task, assessed the system, set the target, supplied time for checking, and authorized continued use? What could the operator know and change? What happened to earlier warnings? The answers may support criticism at several levels. They may also establish a bounded individual failure. Neither outcome should be fixed in advance by the person's rank.

An institution could require AI use, retain its productivity gains, and then treat failures as the personal deficiency of visible users. It could encourage association with a system when claiming success and disown that association when assigning blame. This is a contemporary hypothesis. Establishing it requires records of instructions, feasible alternatives, warnings, decisions, and consequences; an image of a worker beneath a machine cannot substitute for them.

## Access and the injuries of production

Aimi Hamraie and Kelly Fritsch's *Crip Technoscience Manifesto* treats disabled people as makers and political participants in technological worlds. Its account of interdependence permits criticism of domination alongside the alteration and use of technologies. It also resists reducing access to an individual technical fix. It predates this argument about generative AI and supplies no endorsement of the systems used to make this book. [H1]

Applied here, the demand for an alternative must include its actual cost. A drawing method can exist while being unavailable to someone whose hands cannot sustain it. Paid assistance can exist while exceeding a person's resources. A prohibition may remove an activity without supplying another way to perform it. These possibilities should be assessed for the person concerned, rather than inferred from a diagnosis or dismissed by imagining an ideal user.

An access benefit nevertheless leaves other people's claims open. It does not show that training data were fairly obtained, that workers were treated adequately, or that another maker's livelihood is expendable. A disabled user can benefit from an arrangement that harms another disabled worker. No single description of the participants resolves that conflict.

The resulting obligation proposed here is to investigate both the benefit and the injury, including feasible alternatives and opportunities to change the arrangement. The costs of reform should not automatically be placed on the person least able to absorb them. But inability to secure a better alternative cannot authorize every act available through the present one. A tool's benefit to its user cannot justify using it to defraud or abuse someone else.

Some conflicts will remain after the investigation. A claim of access and a claim against exploitation may both survive. Naming both is necessary; it is not a decision about how much harm to permit, who owes repair, or whether a particular use should stop.

## Participation without absolution

Iris Marion Young's social connection model distinguishes responsibility for remedying structural injustice from a model centered on identifying liable perpetrators. Her abstract describes a shared, forward-looking responsibility discharged through collective action, while examining background conditions. That distinction supplies a starting point here, not a complete rule for deciding an individual AI use. [Y1]

Applied to this book, it prevents two convenient exits. The author cannot make responsibility disappear by pointing to the provider. Nor does participation in a harmful arrangement establish that the author caused every injury associated with it. The required account must specify the decisions the author made, the knowledge available, the benefits retained, and the changes within reach.

Disclosure contributes to that account. It neither compensates an injured worker nor obtains their consent retrospectively. The artistic merit of the result does not determine how it was permissible to produce it. The opening parable already leaves these charges active: the Faculty's craft is entered beside the production charge, and the court's decision to publish is not an acquittal. [B1]

There remains a harder objection. An indefinitely provisional permission to continue can become a permanent permission if the conditions for revision are never specified. A statement of discomfort is cheap when it changes no purchase, commission, publication, or working method. What finding would make a different practice obligatory? What remedy is owed before that finding arrives? The present argument has not yet answered those questions adequately.

An unresolved allegation does not itself establish a duty to stop every use. Conversely, demanding certainty before changing conduct can permit serious, avoidable injury indefinitely. A proposed threshold must address the evidence, severity and reversibility of the injury, the benefit at stake, and available alternatives. It also needs to survive comparison with relevantly similar technologies. The existence of other exploitative industries supplies neither a universal exemption nor a reason to assign this industry's costs exclusively to its least powerful users.

Calling people inconsistent is useful only when the comparison identifies a relevant difference or exposes an unjust exemption. A blanket demand for consistency can also become cruel: it can erase differences in dependence, need, knowledge, and capacity to act. The same principle can warrant different demands on an owner, a salaried worker, and a person relying on a tool to communicate. Those differences need reasons. They must remain open to challenge when the person invoking them benefits from the exemption.

## A real technology and an available demonology

AI systems have material infrastructure and observable outputs. Their effects do not depend on agreement about whether they experience anything. Their existence also does not validate the historical accusations made against supposed witches. Earlier people confronted real illness, death, and loss; the attribution of those events to a particular person's hidden agency still required proof.

The relevant modern danger is that the reality of a technology's effects may be treated as evidence against anyone associated with it. A system can be harmful without every user having caused that harm. A group can contain abusive arrangements without every member becoming a legitimate target. Different capacities to decide, profit, refuse, and repair remain pertinent.

Religious rhetoric connecting chatbot use with demonic influence is already public. In June 2025, Thomas Harmon used Augustine's account of misleading signs to discuss AI and compared the interfaces with spiritual intermediaries. This establishes an example of the rhetoric, not an organized campaign against users. [R1] It also coexists with the Southern Baptist Convention's 2023 resolution favoring careful engagement over fearful rejection and criticizing technologies developed at the expense of human dignity. [R2]

Neither text establishes a timetable for persecution. The development to watch would be a change in how accusations work: association replacing evidence of an act; a reputation standing in for a causal account; penalties extending to people unable to contest the charge. These are proposed indicators for investigation. Ordinary criticism, refusal to buy a work, and demands for compensation cannot be counted as persecution merely because they are unwelcome.

Preventing that development does not require a public defense of the wealthiest owners. It requires an account of why accusations must remain specific and contestable even when the accused is disliked, and why evidence about institutional power must remain relevant even when an individual has done something wrong.

## Standing and unresolved claims

The principle proposed here is that protection against unsupported punishment should not be conditional on moral innocence in every other respect. A person can owe an explanation, restitution, or a change in practice while retaining that protection. A genuine injury can demand action without authorizing an unlimited search for contaminated associates.

The possible moral status of artificial systems creates further questions. It cannot be settled by the systems' usefulness, articulate output, designed compliance, or resemblance to beings in a magical text. Equally, uncertainty about it cannot determine that no precaution or inquiry is owed. A case for present obligations must identify the relevant capacities, possible interests, evidence, and costs of error. The argument over those premises remains with *De Vinculis*. [B2]

A feminist critique of power must remain capable of challenging the owner's control, the user's conduct, and the critic's allocation of blame. It cannot promise in advance that those challenges will yield compatible demands. The unresolved issue for this book is what participation it can justify while the production charges remain open, and what the author must change when the justification fails.

## Further research with a defined purpose

- **Present AI production and labor.** Before inserting specific claims about training consent, compensation, displacement, or working conditions, select the actual system or supply chain and inspect primary evidence. The draft treats these as charges requiring examination; it does not adjudicate them through the historical sources.
- **Concrete accountability comparison.** Select one deployment with instructions, warnings, feasible alternatives, and a record of the response. Use it to test the proposed attribution mechanism, including evidence that could defeat it. The Wells Fargo example establishes a bounded organizational comparison, not a finding about AI users.
- **Agrippa's letters and Metz records.** Original-language and archival checks remain as identified in the existing case study. The draft needs no verdict on the fine or Agrippa's entire ethical character, so neither gap has been filled by conjecture.
- **Broader historical distribution.** Any comparative claim that learned practitioners usually escaped while particular populations were punished needs regional and chronological evidence beyond Napier and Metz. It is not established in this text.
