# Review of the scapegoating research

**Reviewed:** 18 October 2026, by Codex, at the author's request  
**Input:** the three research files introduced in PR #3, merge `c7953e1`, and their cited manuscript passages  
**Scope:** assessment of the research and focused corrections; placement in the bound book remains undecided

## What could defeat the proposed connection

An inaccurate attribution, an unjust sanction, a coerced confession and suppression of a warning need not have the same cause or serve the same institutional purpose. Their resemblance in the book cannot establish one scapegoat mechanism. A case needs evidence of the attribution process, what it displaced, the authority to act on it, and the consequences of that substitution. Evidence that the attribution matched the person's contribution would challenge that account.

The register offers candidate mechanisms to investigate: selecting the first observer from a record; producing apparent corroboration through interrogation or repetition; and protecting an arrangement by attributing its failures to users. These remain distinguishable proposals. Describing an institution's function does not establish an actor's intention.

## The register's readings

The comparison between CULPAX and the review rules is between a depicted failure and proposed safeguards. It is not a contradiction merely because the passages prescribe different conduct. The research question is when an institution actually uses the depicted rule. The register now states that distinction.

Two other interpretations exceeded their supporting passages. The general fact that reports have conditions of production establishes no reliability floor for coerced confessions. Reversing a totalizing story also differs from assigning responsibility to an executive on evidence. Those readings have been narrowed. The proposed protocol mapping remains provisional until its fields are checked against the actual specification.

The demand side remains open. Who requests a name, what consequence follows from supplying one, and which alternative explanation or repair becomes harder to pursue? These questions need records of a particular case. Relief, unanimity, institutional survival and deliberate deception should not be treated as interchangeable answers.

## Metz and the historical claims

The case study separates the intervention, the release, belief attribution and causal credit. Its remaining access limits matter. Multiple historians reading one correspondence are not multiple independent witnesses to the event. Brennonius is a second correspondent whose knowledge must be assessed separately for each assertion; a shared publication route alone does not settle dependence.

The directly retrieved text of [Hoorens and Renders (2012)](https://hansrendersarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/sixteenth-century-renders.pdf), printed pp. 7–9, supports their account of the jurisdictional, witness and baptism arguments. It also marks the evidential limits around Agrippa's later interventions. Their inference from the absence of preserved further action to his motives deserves the same scrutiny as the rescue narrative. No independently checked court record or original Latin letter has been added by this review.

The study's statement that the Latin would settle the fine's grounds has been softened. A letter may report one ground, both grounds, or an incomplete understanding of the decision. Even a clear reading would establish what the correspondent says before it establishes why the court acted. The fine remains relevant evidence with an unresolved basis.

The route figure now distinguishes a petition's requested outcome from a demonstrated cause of release. It also uses shorter labels that render without embedded HTML.

## Modern cases and the causal target

The [Mitsubishi judgment](https://www.courts.go.jp/english/Judgments/search/1140/index.html), especially majority sections 2 and 3(1), was inspected directly. The case notes now limit the finding to the causal target the judgment addresses. The original all-four screening verdict has been downgraded; the corporate-loss claim remains unestablished. A primary record's authority does not transfer to an additional outcome it did not assess.

The [Wells Fargo deferred prosecution agreement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/press-release/file/1251336/dl), Exhibit A, paragraphs 28–31, was also checked. It supports the distinction between leadership's individual-misconduct account and the sales model, with disciplinary referrals and terminations concentrated at branch level. It does not establish the innocence of every dismissed employee or quantify losses caused specifically by that attribution. Other screened cases have not received a fresh source audit.

T2 needs an explicit outcome and intermediate links: attribution, response, continued exposure, further harm, and organizational loss. Evidence for one link cannot automatically complete the rest. T3 needs its additional comparison with a feasible repair path. A sudden loss alone does not supply that counterfactual.

The present screen selects for severe organizational losses. It therefore cannot estimate how often scapegoating occurs, whether it generally preserves organizations, or how often its principal costs remain with those accused. Those questions require a different case selection.
