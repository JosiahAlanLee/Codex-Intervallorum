 Systematology against
 public incident reports
 Test setup and executed retrospective pilot
 10 October 2026 | Version 0.1 | Six incidents, five organizations

 Operationally usable. Incremental advantage over competent SRE
 remains unestablished.

 This evaluates the operational framing of the protocol. Its strongest failure risk is that it
 relabels familiar reliability practices while the distinct claims about power, standing and
 correction remain unmeasured. A convincing account of a known outage is not evidence that
 the protocol would have anticipated it.

 What was actually run
 The full 24-page Revision 3 and its one-page condensation were recovered. Six primary
 operator reports linked from Dan Luu's collection were inspected. Each received
 source-located coding, paired SRE and Systematology interpretations, a candidate additional
 question and an inference explicitly rejected. A Python runner checked the coding and
 recalculated the descriptive results. [P1, P2, I1, S1-S6]

 One analyst wrote both readings with the outcomes visible. There were zero independent
 model runs, zero independent judges and zero fitted causal models. The local design
 was frozen after reading the index descriptions but before opening these primary reports in
 this session; prior familiarity with famous outages remains. This is not external
 preregistration.

 Completed coding results
 • 3 of 6: relevant prior exercises documented, without establishing full incident-equivalent
 coverage; 2 of 6: explicit testing gaps; 1 of 6: rehearsal history unknown. These are
 categories, not a preparedness ranking.

 • 3 of 6: incident-related dependencies affected response tools or status communication.
 The other three were not assessed on this narrow variable; they are not negative controls.

 • 6 of 6: reporting-retaliation regimes and metric-gaming mechanisms remain unknown;
 suitable ownership comparisons and recurrence follow-up are unavailable.

 The paired readings cover substantially the same operational mechanisms. No independently established
 Systematology-only finding emerged. This is a limitation of the experiment, not proof of equivalence. The
 code, evidence ledger and 18 unrun development prompts accompany this report.

 Protocol, comparator and scope
 Primary version recovered
 The source is Systematology: A revisable systems practice, Revision 3, October 2026, 24
 pages, especially §§3-5, 7, 9, 15-16, 22-24 and Part IX. Its one-page condensation supplies
 four audit gaps and three change rules. The slow-clocks packet supplies additional
 hypotheses; those are not silently substituted for the protocol's six empirical bets. [P1-P3]

 What the operational adaptation asks
 • Can an alternative actually be used during the specified failure, by the people who need it?

 • Can responders discover inadequacy and act on it, or does the system disable its own
 means of correction?

 • What testing would count as failure for a successor? Does introducing an option later
 remove an existing one?

 • Whose interests and irreversible losses are omitted from the recovery objective? Who can
 contest the decision?

 • Is there a demonstrated feedback or incentive mechanism behind a misleading metric, or
 only an ordinary measurement defect?

 A competent baseline
 The comparator includes common-mode dependency analysis, safe changes, recovery tests,
 usable emergency access, operator workload, communication, customer impact, integrity and
 organizational learning. It includes blameless reporting and corrective-action follow-through.
 Google's SRE guidance explicitly includes these human and organizational concerns; a
 technical-only comparator would be too weak. [B1, B2]

 Where the adaptation must not change the claim
 Technical independence is not governance independence. Separate power, DNS,
 identity and management paths may survive a failure while remaining under one owner.
 Revision 3's bypass test additionally demands independence in ownership, funding, standards
 and upstream dependencies, with affordable access. These reports can expose technical
 coupling; that does not validate the stronger ownership proposition.

 Restoration is not automatically revision. A changed database topology, rollback or
 temporary bypass does not establish a change to institutional goals or decision rights.
 Technical changes, organizational changes and their implementation status need separate
 fields.

 Unknown is not absent. A report's failure to mention retaliation, an appeal route or a
 metric incentive cannot establish that none exists. Conversely, a public postmortem is not
 proof that internal reporting is protected.

 Case readings: recovery routes
 AWS2017 | Amazon Web Services | 2017-02-28
 A maintenance command removed excessive capacity, forcing dependent S3 subsystems to
 restart. Large-region full restarts had not occurred for years; recovery at current scale
 exceeded expectations. S3 dependence blocked status-console updates; alternate messaging
 remained available. Command safeguards and multiregion status administration were
 changed. [S1]

 SRE reading. Bound destructive commands and validate recovery at current scale. Keep
 incident communication outside the affected failure domain.

 Protocol reading. A nominal repair path needs a scoped test (§4). An effective bypass must
 remain reachable (§7); technical separation does not establish ownership independence.

 Boundary. A stale status display establishes neither metric gaming nor suppression of reporting.

 GL2017 | GitLab | 2017-01-31
 During replication repair, the primary data directory was mistakenly deleted. Logical backups
 failed; email failure notifications were rejected. A staging snapshot enabled recovery but left
 a data-loss interval. Replication and snapshots did not constitute interchangeable recovery
 options. The report attributes absent regular backup testing to unassigned ownership. [S2]

 SRE reading. Verify recoverability and failure notification delivery; assign an owner. Protect
 destructive operations and preserve data integrity separately from uptime.

 Protocol reading. Nominal alternatives do not prove usable options (§7). Separate restored
 function from irreversible loss and responsibility for correction (§§9,16).

 Boundary. Undelivered automated email is not evidence of retaliation against human reporters.

 CF2019 | Cloudflare | 2019-07-02
 A globally deployed WAF expression exhausted CPU even in simulation mode; normal staged
 software rollout did not apply. Authentication failure obstructed control access; an
 infrequently used bypass enabled termination. Traffic returned before full WAF restoration.
 Passing functional tests did not cover runaway CPU use. [S3]

 SRE reading. Stage risky changes; test computational resource limits. Exercise emergency
 access and track protection as well as traffic.

 Protocol reading. Successful tests validate only their declared scope (§4). Correction
 capacity can depend on the system it corrects (§§7,22).

 Boundary. A test blind spot does not establish Goodhart effects or an incentive to game a metric.

 All source claims are operator self-reports. The paired readings are retrospective interpretations by one
 analyst, not independent predictions. Exact locators, uncertainty and the unanswered additional questions
 are in cases.json.

 Case readings: objectives and scope
 GH2018 | GitHub | 2018-10-21
 A brief partition triggered cross-region failover and divergent writes, making simple failback
 unsafe. Responders prioritized data integrity. Backup restoration was tested daily, but the
 full-cluster recovery situation was unprecedented. Reconciliation remained underway at
 publication; further failover constraints and failure-scenario testing were proposed. [S4]

 SRE reading. Validate application compatibility with automated failover topologies. Score
 integrity and availability separately; rehearse full recovery.

 Protocol reading. A changed configuration can preserve one goal while failing another
 (§§3,18). Revision is not inherently better; irreversible loss changes the recovery judgment
 (§16).

 Boundary. Longer recovery alone cannot establish worse preparedness, and fail-forward is not constitutional
 revision.

 FB2021 | Facebook | 2021-10-04
 A backbone command and failed command check disconnected sites; DNS withdrawals also
 disabled investigation tools. Normal and out-of-band access failed; secure physical access
 delayed repair. Earlier drills helped controlled restart, although the total backbone loss had
 not been exercised. Security and recovery speed conflicted. [S5]

 SRE reading. Test loss of primary and emergency management paths together. Balance
 secure access with recovery, and rehearse staged reintroduction.

 Protocol reading. A claimed bypass must function during the specified failure (§7). Maintain
 correction capacity without assuming that every access barrier should be removed (§22).

 Boundary. Security delaying recovery is not by itself evidence of illegitimate authority or a benefit from
 changing ownership.

 CF2023 | Cloudflare | 2023-11-02
 Services depended on components outside the replicated cluster. Tests had not removed the
 entire affected facility. Recovery coverage differed by product; some required improvised
 restoration. A deliberate rest period delayed full recovery. Most control functions recovered
 before all services; edge traffic continued. Stronger recovery requirements were announced.
 [S6]

 SRE reading. Test whole-facility loss and actual product recovery coverage. Include
 responder fatigue and endpoint-specific impact in recovery assessment.

 Protocol reading. Local redundancy does not establish end-to-end alternative routes (§7).
 Capability and recovery labels require scoped evidence (§4); care complicates pure time
 minimization (§§13,18).

 Boundary. Slower full recovery is not sufficient evidence of poorer governance; rest and service mix affect
 the endpoint.

 All source claims are operator self-reports. The paired readings are retrospective interpretations by one
 analyst, not independent predictions. Exact locators, uncertainty and the unanswered additional questions
 are in cases.json.

 Which hypotheses can this corpus test?
 The current run tests coding feasibility. None of the six empirical bets has an identified
 comparative effect estimate here. The missing information is substantive, not a problem
 solved by collecting a few more famous postmortems.

 C1 | Punishment and forecast accuracy
 Needs observed reporting regimes, repeated forecasts and outcomes. Corporate tone cannot
 establish protection; a failed notification channel is not retaliation.

 C2 | Commons governance and persistence
 Outside the sampling domain. Corporate outages are not a comparison of commons under
 alternative governance regimes.

 C3 | Targets drifting from the intended property
 Needs incentive or deployment changes, repeated review cycles and independent outcome
 measures. Blind spots in tests do not establish metric gaming.

 C4 | Bottleneck ownership and correction
 Needs independently measured control arrangements and comparable dependent systems.
 Common-mode technical failure is not a causal ownership comparison.

 C5 | Additive versus substitutive change
 Needs actual option availability over time, reversal cost and persistent conflict, with
 comparable interventions. Rollback alone is not this outcome.

 C6 | Experimentation plus protected reporting
 Needs both exposures and longitudinal outcomes. Public evidence of local experimentation
 does not establish a protected reporting regime.

 Slow-clocks extensions
 Fallback rehearsal: record date, scale, tested failure, dependency coverage and authority
 to execute. Prepared/unprepared is too crude. Durable memory versus message volume:
 requires linked incident histories and actual artifact use. Alert saturation: requires alert
 streams, labels and responder load. Adversarial timing: requires intrusion times, staffing
 and time-at-risk denominators. The six selected cases do not provide these comparisons. [P3]

 Do not pool recovery times across unlike endpoints. The data preserve approximate versus exact timing,
 restoration versus residual data loss, and partial versus full service recovery. Intentional choices to protect
 integrity or avoid fatigue can lengthen a clock without demonstrating worse correction.

 The next comparison is ready to stage
 Three conditions; the primary comparison is B versus C
 A: competent SRE review. B: SRE plus operational Systematology. C: SRE plus a generic
 extended checklist. B and C additions contain 199 and 205 words; actual tokenizer counts
 remain to be measured. Each condition uses the same packet, at most five findings and a
 450-word output cap.

 What is included now
 The bundle contains 18 fresh-context prompt files, a shuffled job manifest, private
 condition/outcome crosswalk, a JSON response format and structural response validation. All
 jobs are explicitly marked not run. The six packets are sparse retrospective reconstructions
 for development only. Their source reports shaped what was included, and their outcomes
 may be familiar to a model. They are not a predictive benchmark.

 What an independent run requires
 • New cases, selected from a frozen eligible corpus. Record access exclusions and duplicates.
 Keep organization clusters visible.

 • Sufficient independently dated pre-incident documentation. If unavailable, keep that case
 retrospective; do not relabel a censored postmortem as prospective evidence.

 • One fresh isolated context per case and condition. Supply only the assigned prompt and
 evidence. Retain model/version/settings and raw replies; prohibit browsing and outside
 memory as evidence.

 • Two independent technical judges, blind to condition where possible. Save individual scores
 before adjudication. Condition vocabulary may weaken blinding.

 • Score specific, supported and actionable mechanisms. Report contradicted and
 unassessable claims separately. A mechanism omitted from a postmortem is not
 automatically false.

 How the protocol could lose
 It could add no useful supported findings relative to the equally extended SRE checklist, or
 add enough unsupported governance claims, complexity or review effort to outweigh gains.
 Set the smallest useful gain and acceptable error burden before confirmation. The proposed
 24-case extension is for measurement development; it is not a power calculation.

 What the completed run warrants
 Keep the protocol available as an audit checklist. Its usability on incident narratives is
 demonstrated at the level of one analyst's application. Its superiority, reliability across coders
 and causal governance claims remain open. A null advantage would narrow the operational
 claim without settling the normative commitments or the biological analogies.
