## Scope and attribution

Sledge’s scholarship can inform a charitable reader without predicting his verdict on the book, particularly its AI ethics. This document assesses the manuscript against relevant work by Sledge. Objections and proposed revisions are the reviewer’s unless explicitly attributed. It is not a review written by Sledge or evidence of his endorsement.

The comparison includes the active consolidated opening, *Pseudomonarchia Escalationum*, *Clavicula Graduum*, *Ars Notoria Stigmatum*, the Tarot’s declared symbolism status, the *Clavis* and *De Vinculis*, and the new parables *De Officio Accusatoris* and *De Matribus Noctis*. Archived source editions are not treated as the active bound text.

Evidence inspected includes the available public draft and published abstract of Sledge’s Dee article, the abstract of his dissertation, relevant ESOTERICA episode descriptions and reading lists, interview text, and his public presentation of contemporary incantation bowls. This is a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive audit of his publications or full lecture catalogue. The final journal text of the Dee article and the full dissertation were not inspected; complete lectures were not watched for this review. Those limits qualify the findings below.

## 1. Highest-priority revision: “there was no original” in *De Matribus Noctis*

Sledge’s Lilith material distinguishes ancient antecedents, later Jewish legends, and medieval Kabbalistic elaborations. The parable’s account of successive, incompatible constructions largely fits that historical approach. His recent presentation also emphasizes linguistic accidents and the importance of a text parodying rabbinic legends. [S1–S3]

The manuscript nevertheless makes a stronger inference:

> “One charge cannot be brought against them, that they corrupt the original, because there was no original to corrupt.”

**This does not follow.** A composite tradition can contain particular objects, texts, and practices that can be misrepresented. Failure to establish a single primordial goddess does not establish the absence of identifiable sources or obligations toward them.

The absence of an established unified prototype and the absence of particular antecedents are different claims. Likewise, showing that historical makers borrowed, transformed, or recombined material does not by itself settle the terms under which a present maker should do so.

| Passage or device | Problem | Smallest repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “There was no original to corrupt.” | Plural origins do not eliminate particular sources. | Limit the claim to the absence of an established, unified prototype. Leave the fidelity of particular borrowings open to examination. |
| “A word none of them could read.” | Different translations do not demonstrate an inability to read the word. | Describe disagreement over its referent or interpretation. |
| King James owls becoming evidence for identifying the plaque. | The manuscript’s source register explicitly identifies this reception story as invented. | Make its conjectural or fictional status apparent beside the passage. Do not let it stand as a documented explanation of a scholar’s identification. |

The blanket **[F]** designation permits fiction, but historical particulars still help persuade the reader. The distinction needs to remain visible where the argument depends on them. The issue is not that a parable contains invented speakers; it is whether an invented episode supplies the evidence for an argument about actual reception or transmission.

**Relevant manuscript locations:** *De Matribus Noctis*, III and VII; source register entries Mat/M8 and the related identification history.

## 2. Dee and Kelley: a useful connection and a methodological tension

In “Between Loagaeth and Cosening” (*Aries*, 2010), Sledge challenges the simple choice between supernatural revelation and deliberate fraud. He develops an explanation involving cultural circumstances, altered states, possible psychiatric vulnerability, and myth-making sincerely experienced as revelation. This bears on the book’s distinction between constructing something and knowingly deceiving someone. [S4]

The friction concerns confidence. The public draft sometimes advances fairly specific retrospective psychiatric possibilities for Kelley, while acknowledging that direct examination is impossible. The manuscript’s distinction between recorded testimony and inferred mental state calls for greater restraint here. **An explanatory hypothesis can be useful without establishing a diagnosis.** Retain his challenge to the fraud/revelation binary while leaving those diagnoses unresolved. [S5]

**Relevant manuscript locations:** *Clavis*, “What kind of sentence is this?” and “How to inspect the claims”; *Ars Notoria Stigmatum*, the separation of manufacture from authentication.

## 3. *Ars Notoria*: the modern adaptation changes the historical promise

Sledge describes the medieval work as Christian theurgy involving purification, prayer, unknown words, and inspection of notae, promising supernaturally infused knowledge. It concerns more than an arrangement of suggestive images. [S6]

The current *Ars Notoria Stigmatum* explicitly changes that promise and identifies its exercises as inventions. That distinction should stay prominent.

The charitable historical objection would be: **What has been preserved when the theological account of knowledge has been replaced?** Figures and disciplined attention remain; the historical explanation of why they work has changed. The adaptation can be worthwhile without constituting a reconstruction or an explanation of the historical practice.

There is also a caution in the other direction. Anthony Metivier’s account of his discussion with Sledge proposes possible concentration or memory benefits, but explicitly presents this as speculation. Manuscript popularity does not establish efficacy. The book’s pending comparison with an ordinary checklist or reading procedure remains necessary. [S7]

**Relevant manuscript locations:** “Collation of the surviving hands”; “Reading leaf — the work still owed by the fields”; “Objection left with the condemned leaf”; “Three appointments of the Keeper.”

## 4. Trithemius: decipherment does not exhaust the object

Sledge’s presentation situates the *Steganographia* within a project combining cryptology, astrology, alchemical theory, prophecy, and spiritual illumination. [S8]

This matters for *Graduum*: successfully decoding a cipher does not, by itself, establish that its religious setting was disposable camouflage. Conversely, a religious setting does not prevent an inscription from performing a precise technical operation.

The book’s working ciphers make this comparison concrete. Their recoverable messages, visual effects, and ritual presentation can be examined separately. **Keep that separation; avoid making decipherment a final explanation of the entire object.** The current prose mostly observes this already.

This is an inferred interpretive caution, not an allegation that the current manuscript has made the stronger claim.

**Relevant manuscript locations:** *Clavicula Graduum*, IX, “The Square Table,” and X, “The Prayer of the Office.”

## 5. Divine authority retains a theological remainder

*De Vinculis*, Commentarium II, already acknowledges that Iamblichus gives several explanations of apparent command. That qualification matters: divine investiture and participation in divine unity can change what “commander” and “commanded” mean.

The reviewer’s historically informed objection is that an office credential explains only part of the comparison. A ritual may claim to transform the operator’s relation to divinity, rather than simply authorize an otherwise unchanged individual. This concern should not be attributed to Sledge as a stated judgment on the manuscript.

The most useful verification upgrade would be checking those passages against Clarke, Dillon, and Hershbell’s modern translation. The current register relies on Thomas Taylor. The modern edition presents theurgy within Iamblichus’s account of union with the gods, which is precisely the context the administrative analogy risks compressing. [S9]

Treat this as a verification priority. The review does not establish an error in the current interpretation of IV.2–3.

**Relevant manuscript locations:** *De Vinculis*, Commentarium II, “Of the Ring”; VII/S1 in the source register.

## 6. Marx, reification, and the limits of corrective procedure

The abstract of Sledge’s dissertation, *Freedom, Solidarity, Revolution: Marx and the Ethical* (2014), places reification—the inversion of relations between subjects and objects—at the centre of its interpretation. It develops a historically situated ethic of solidarity. The full dissertation was not inspected. [S10]

Working from that framing, the question for the book becomes:

**Can an institution become more responsive and correctable while preserving the social relations that make people into instruments?**

The opening book permits this possibility, and *Escalationum*’s extraction of the repairer’s knowledge gives it a concrete form. Further development should examine ownership, wages, dependence, and control over the purpose of work alongside access to correction.

That would extend the argument. It would not demonstrate that the book’s adopted commitments and Sledge’s ethical account have the same foundation. Declaring care a commitment does not settle the separate question of its philosophical justification or historical conditions.

**Relevant manuscript locations:** *Clavicula Matricis et Intervallorum*, I and its declared commitments; *Pseudomonarchia Escalationum*, “The extraction of the hand” and “The workshop record”; *De Vinculis*, Commentarium VI, “Of the Cabinet.”

## 8. A precedent for the charitable reader

Sledge’s own bookshop presents contemporary incantation bowls made with ceramic artist Dee Dee Mangum: historical imagery, a newly composed Aramaic inscription, and an accompanying translation, explicitly offered as contemporary objects based on historical examples. [S13]

This provides evidence that historical scholarship and newly made esoteric art can coexist. The reviewer’s inference is that a charitable scholarly reader can accept invented objects while asking for accurate attribution, recoverable distinctions, and adequate evidence wherever they make historical claims. It supplies no verdict on the separate question of AI production.

## 9. Handoff for the Agrippa / Witch(x) discussion

**Placement context:** The author reports that Claude is developing a Witch(x) discussion of Agrippa’s defense of an accused witch. That draft has not been inspected for this handoff. The following is an editorial proposal for that discussion, not a finding about its current contents or a fresh historical reconstruction of the case.

The strongest reason the proposed connection could fail is that a defense in one proceeding cannot, by itself, establish the defender’s complete beliefs, motives, or general political commitments. It also does not establish the opposite of those propositions. A humane intervention and objectionable metaphysical assumptions could coexist if the sources support both; neither should be invented to produce a balanced portrait.

That makes the Agrippa discussion a suitable place to distinguish the following questions:

| Question | Evidence required | Inference to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What intervention is actually recorded? | Identify the witness, date, genre, provenance, and any relevant transmission or editorial mediation. Separate contemporary records from later retellings. | Treating a later summary as unmediated access to the event. |
| What argument did the defense use? | Quote or closely analyze the relevant legal, procedural, theological, or evidentiary argument from the inspected source. | Turning a bounded argument against a prosecution into a universal rejection of witchcraft or demonology. |
| What did Agrippa believe? | Assess independently relevant statements, their contexts, and their relation to the case. Preserve the distinction between a recorded statement and a belief attribution. | Inferring disbelief solely from defense, or belief solely from the vocabulary available to a legal argument. |
| Why did he intervene? | Distinguish stated reasons from reconstructed motives. Allow multiple motives where warranted and uncertainty where they are not recoverable. | Treating professional, personal, or strategic interests as proof of insincerity; treating an admirable action as proof of exclusively humanitarian motives. |
| What does the case show about the prosecution? | Identify the actual evidentiary and procedural issues documented in the case. | Using the case as a complete explanation of all persecutions. |
| What modern comparison is being proposed? | Name the relation being compared and what evidence would establish or defeat it. | Allowing historical resemblance to supply a finding about present AI experience, welfare, or institutional legitimacy. |

The discussion can use Sledge as an interlocutor without making him an authority that settles the reconstruction. His work is especially relevant to the distinction between a constructed account and deliberate deception, but it should undergo the same scrutiny as other scholarship.

The key methodological question is **how far an explanation is warranted by its evidence**. Possible failure modes include stopping at an undifferentiated “unknown,” choosing the most coherent explanation without discriminating evidence, and turning one documented intervention into a unified portrait of its author. Those problems need separate treatment; they are not resolved by selecting one general attitude toward uncertainty.

### Suggested editorial operations

1. Preserve the current separations among textual prescription, historical practice, reported experience, belief attribution, empirical outcome, and normative commitment.
2. Use the Agrippa case to demonstrate those separations on actual witnesses before applying them to broader comparisons.
3. State any inferred motive or mental state at the strength the evidence warrants. Do not upgrade a plausible account merely because it is charitable or critical.
4. Address the retrospective-diagnosis issue as a methodological question. Do not imply that rejecting a diagnosis establishes supernatural causation or deliberate fraud.
5. Make the independent correction to *De Matribus Noctis*. A later methodological discussion cannot supply the missing inference in “there was no original.”
6. Verify historically consequential background claims against the relevant full passages. An honest disclosure that only an abstract or summary was consulted identifies unfinished work; it does not complete it.
