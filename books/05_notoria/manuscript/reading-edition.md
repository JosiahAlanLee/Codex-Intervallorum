## The leaf found without its reader

<!-- leaf: plate; art: found; cipher: THE TEAR IS NOT A SIGN -->

A leaf survives with one corner missing. Three rings remain legible; the tracks within them terminate at the loss. Nothing in the surviving lines tells whether they continued. The red thread crosses the outer ring and ends at the surviving edge.

The found leaf and its damage were composed for this gathering.

## Collation of the surviving hands

<!-- leaf: text -->

The first hand drew a circle. The second made an entrance. The third objected that the entrance had been mistaken for a mouth.

We have retained all three.

The present gathering contains three arts: the placing of lots, the changing of lines, and the addressing of a remembered corpus. Each has an oration. Each also has a reading leaf, where the other hand interrupts. Their figures belong to this gathering; they have not been recovered from the traditions discussed beside them.

The first hand speaks as though the book had been found. The second records its manufacture. Neither is permitted to authenticate the other.

The Company of the Missing Corner, the Keepers of the Unturned Leaf and the Assembly at the Unoccupied Place are this gathering’s invented copyists. Their changing names give the same unstable literary device three signatures.

The medieval Ars notoria supplies the association of figures and knowledge-seeking orations. This gathering alters that promise. It offers no acquisition of an art by inspection alone. The critical edition has not been consulted in full for this draft; its ritual particulars are therefore not reconstructed here. [S1]

The found-document voice borrows from the CCRU. Its invented history is part of the literary form.

*Another hand:* A missing source is not a licence to invent its testimony. An invented source is not a missing source.

## Rule for approaching a nota

<!-- leaf: text -->

Set down the question before uncovering the figure. Leave enough room beside it to record the question you discover you actually asked.

Name the operation. Was a card drawn, a line generated, an inscription copied, a path deposited, or a passage recalled? If several operations occurred, keep their order. A copy of the result cannot always recover the act that made it.

Name the reader's contribution. A position may have been assigned before the draw; a correspondence may have been chosen afterwards; an example may have been omitted because it would not fit. These are acts in the reading. They belong in its account.

When two readings disagree, put them beside the unchanged figure. If the figure does not decide between them, do not make a third reading whose sole virtue is to end the disagreement.

The Keeper of Intervals has been given three appointments in this book: before the shuffle, between the changing line and its explanation, and before the record is erased. These appointments are inventions of the present workshop, not timings recovered from Tarot, the Yijing, Ifá, or a medieval nota.

At each appointment leave a blank. The blank is for an answer that the apparatus cannot give. It is not a chamber in which an answer waits invisibly.

*Another hand:* The rule can itself become a ceremony that excuses poor work. A completed record does not prove that the observation was good.

## Oration of the Company of the Missing Corner

<!-- leaf: oration -->

Give the image a neighbour and it will acquire a story.

Give it another neighbour and the former story will show its seams. We ask to see those seams while the cloth is still upon the table.

Let the place be named before the lot arrives. Let the lot arrive without a change to the place. If the place is altered, let the alteration stand beside it, in the same ink as the interpretation.

We have seen a gate called a departure when it stood after a traveller, and an obstruction when it stood before one. The gate did not move. The reader crossed the table.

We have seen a crown called the summit of an ascent and the weight that bent the ascending neck. Neither account was written upon the crown. The accounts may be useful; their usefulness does not make them inscriptions.

Let the excluded card remain in the reckoning. Let the card which would spoil the story be imaginable before the story is complete. Let us say what we would have read had it appeared.

We ask no ancient warrant for this arrangement. A new game may disclose a new relation. It need not borrow an older birthday.

When the last lot has been turned, leave one place without an image. Do not call it the hidden trump. It is the space in which the reader must answer for the reading.

*In the outer margin:* The neighbour was chosen.

## Reading leaf — places, ranks, correspondences

<!-- leaf: text -->

Three orders must be kept distinguishable. A pack can have a rank used in play, a printed numbering, and an arrangement adopted for interpretation. Agreement between two does not make them the same operation.

The brief proposed that later correspondences broke a single native order. The research instead describes regional orders and variant practices. Those historical tables still need checking against the cited monographs. This draft does not turn their reported variation into a proof that earlier readers lacked symbolic intentions.

One change has a readily inspectable witness. In his account of Strength, Waite acknowledges exchanging its place with Justice, ordinarily numbered VIII. He declines to explain his reasons there. That establishes his admitted exchange; that passage alone does not establish the complete Golden Dawn genealogy proposed in the brief. [S2]

Twenty-two images can be paired with twenty-two letters. Equality of the two counts guarantees that bijections exist; it does not select one, date one, or show that a maker intended one. Nor does a later correspondence become worthless simply because it is later. Its historical claim and its artistic use require different assessments.

The arithmetic counts configurations; the historical inquiry asks how particular arrangements were used. Tarot’s game ranks, allegorical meanings and later correspondences require evidence appropriate to each use.

*Another hand:* The oration's gate and crown are our examples. They are not readings attributed to a historical practitioner.

## The reckoning of the lots

<!-- leaf: text -->

Choose a pack of seventy-eight distinct cards and ten labelled places. Draw without replacement. There are seventy-eight choices for the first place, seventy-seven for the next, and so on to sixty-nine for the last.

The product is 4,566,176,969,818,464,000. If each card can additionally occupy either of two distinguished orientations, the number becomes 4,675,765,217,094,107,136,000. The second count assumes that both orientations are admitted independently at each place. It does not establish that any actual shuffle distributes them fairly.

The labelled places have already been counted by the ordered draw. Multiplying again by a number of positions would count the same arrangements twice. A different set of positional meanings defines a different interpretive convention, rather than extra outcomes of this one draw.

The unordered ten-card selection has 1,258,315,963,905 possibilities. It forgets which card occupies which place. The ordered and unordered counts answer different questions.

These are counts of distinguishable configurations under stated rules. They are not counts of meanings. Two arrangements may receive the same reading; one arrangement may sustain several incompatible readings. A large number of configurations does not measure accuracy, insight, or information about an external event.

*Proposed exercise:* Write two incompatible accounts of a three-card arrangement, without changing the positions or replacing a card. Then identify the sentence that each account would have to surrender if a known fact contradicted it. If neither would surrender anything, record that result.

The exercise is ours. It is not an attested rule of cartomancy.

## Oration of the Keepers of the Unturned Leaf

<!-- leaf: oration -->

Let the line change before we explain why it changed.

Let its former place remain visible. We ask for no procession in which the end conceals the steps and then claims to have summoned them.

There are six places. Each admits a division. We can enumerate the arrangements without having learned what any arrangement says.

One reader begins at the foot; another at the head. They may number the same figure differently. Let each set down the direction of reading before the numbers are compared. A quarrel about the labels must not be entered as a discovery about the figures.

The copyist has made a table that runs without a gap. It is a good table for the purpose declared. Beside it lies a book whose leaves are in another order. Let the table remain a table and the book remain available in its order. We need not burn either to demonstrate their difference.

We ask the number to give its proper testimony: which choices have been distinguished, which changes admitted, which weights assigned. We do not ask it to remember an author whose name it has never contained.

If the new order is beautiful, write that it is beautiful. If it helps a calculation, show the calculation. If it is said to be ancient, bring a witness who can speak of its age.

At the last line do not write completion merely because the page is full.

*In the outer margin:* The number does not date the line.

## Reading leaf — a correspondence can be real

<!-- leaf: text -->

It is possible for two arrangements to have the same mathematical structure without one descending from the other. It is also possible for a useful translation to discard something important. Neither possibility decides a historical case in advance.

The edited Leibniz collection lists binary work from 1679 and Bouvet's letter of 4 November 1701. Its chronology supports the narrower point that Leibniz's binary work preceded that letter. This draft inspected the edition's contents, not the original manuscripts or the complete correspondence. [S3]

Maitre's article explicitly argues that the Shao Yong-associated diagram is binary in form and structure, despite differences in construction and reading direction. The abstract therefore cannot be used as evidence that binary was simply imposed on a nonbinary Chinese object. Its historical implications require the full argument. [S4]

The earlier brief's shared history of imposed arithmetic is withdrawn. This leaf does not replace it with the opposite history. Whether a structural correspondence establishes a particular earlier intention is a separate evidentiary question.

The same caution applies to a modern derivation of a sequence. A rule that reproduces every entry establishes reproducibility under that rule. Authorship requires evidence connecting the rule to the makers; uniqueness requires comparison with alternatives; explanatory value requires an account of what the derivation adds.

Keep the received sequence, image-and-number traditions and the modern binary table available in their different uses. A mathematical correspondence establishes a structural relation; a genealogy requires historical witnesses.

*Another hand:* To criticize an imposed reading, we first have to show that it was imposed. Calling a correspondence an imposition does not do that work.

## The reckoning of the changing lines

<!-- leaf: text -->

For this calculation alone, represent each of six lines by one of two states. There are sixty-four figures. Distinguish also whether each line stays or changes. There are then four outcomes per place and 4,096 complete casts. Each cast identifies exactly one ordered pair of primary and resulting figures.

The standard idealized yarrow weights reported in the research packet are 1, 5, 7 and 3 out of sixteen for old yin, young yang, young yin and old yang. We adopt those weights as a model input here; this draft does not verify the physical stalk procedure that is said to yield them. [R0]

In that model the primary yin and yang states each have probability one half. Conditional on a primary yin line, its chance of changing is one eighth; conditional on a primary yang line, three eighths. The unconditional probabilities of those two kinds of change are one sixteenth and three sixteenths. Conditional and unconditional weights must not be substituted for one another.

Assuming independent lines, the primary figures are equally probable. The resulting figures are not: a resulting line is yang with probability three eighths and yin with probability five eighths. By contrast, the usual fair three-coin model gives equal marginal probabilities to both primary and resulting lines.

This is an independently checked consequence of the stated weights. It says nothing about which passages a practitioner should consult when several lines change. A sixty-four by sixty-four array of possible transitions is not a rule for interpretation.

*Another hand:* A regular distribution of beginnings does not guarantee a regular distribution of endings.

## A table of possible endings

Read a row as a primary figure and a column as its resulting figure. Both run from zero to sixty-three in binary order: yin is zero, yang one, and the bottom line is the least significant place. This is a modern arrangement for calculation, not the received sequence or a recovered historical diagram.

Each cell gives the joint weight of that pair under the preceding leaf's idealized yarrow model. The numerator multiplies six line weights; the common denominator is sixteen to the sixth power. The four weights are one, five, seven and three for old yin, young yang, young yin and old yang. Independence is an assumption of the table.

Hatching groups these numerators into seven logarithmic grades, shown below the bars. The grades begin at 1, 7, 49, 343, 2,401, 16,807 and 117,649; the last is the maximum. Twice the hatching does not mean twice the probability. Red outlines mark the unchanged figures along the diagonal.

## Oration of the Assembly at the Unoccupied Place

<!-- leaf: oration -->

Let the mark send us to the speaker without pretending to be the speaker.

Let the speaker send us to the remembered passage without pretending that its whole life has arrived upon the page.

We have brought an index. We have not brought the voices that taught its use, the occasions that altered a telling, or the listener who knew where the account had been shortened. Do not set the index in their chairs.

There are doors enough to number. The numbering does not furnish the rooms. If a door is opened and we cannot read what lies within, let our inability remain ours. Do not describe the room as empty.

We ask to learn the difference between a mark that can be copied and a judgement that must be cultivated. The difference will not always follow the border between a page and a body. A copied sentence may require a teacher; an unwritten account may be argued with exactly.

Let a borrowed term retain the difficulty that brought us to it. Do not carry away its sound and leave its demands with the people from whom it came.

This oration was composed outside the practice named on the reading leaf. It cannot open that practice by declaring itself an entrance.

We leave the last compartment unoccupied. There is no inscription inside which the cipher will reveal. Someone may know what we do not. Their knowledge is not an ornament of our lack.

## Reading leaf — what the index leaves out

<!-- leaf: text -->

UNESCO describes an Odù corpus with 256 divisions and an expanding body of verses. Ayodeji Ogunnaike discusses the organizing power of the figures and proposes that geomantic forms were incorporated into an existing Yoruba practice. He presents this as a historical hypothesis about creative appropriation, not a demonstrated chain from Ifá to Leibniz. [S5, S6]

The arithmetic of eight binary distinctions gives 256 configurations. It does not establish equal likelihood in an instrument, a historical route of transmission, or equivalence between a configuration and the knowledge associated with it. An encoding can be exact within the feature it records while leaving most of a practice unrepresented.

The brief's stronger claims about seniority and the precise ìbò selection rule remain pending checks against Bascom and Abimbola. They do not enter this draft's orations as verified procedure. Nor does failure to find an empirical cast-distribution study establish that nobody has made one.

Inscription and erasure supply the dust-surface comparison. A simulated pheromone field additionally makes density and adjacency affect movement. Establish those relations before claiming a shared mechanism.

The nota diagrams an index. Practitioners’ accounts, the Odù corpus and questions of initiation remain outside this diagram’s scope. No ẹsẹ is reproduced.

*Another hand:* These limits describe the present author's work. They are not an assertion that a living tradition has one universal rule of access.

## The borrowed test

<!-- leaf: text -->

The research summary proposes carrying the distinction between ìmọ̀ and ìgbàgbọ́ into every claim in this book. In the materials actually inspected for that summary, the particular formulation came through a reader's account of Deep Knowledge. It has not yet been checked against the relevant pages of Ogunnaike's book.

The publisher's description confirms a study of knowledge, its acquisition and its verification in Ifá and Sufism. It does not authorize replacing either epistemology with this workshop's preferred rules. The detailed borrowing remains pending. [S7]

We can state our own narrower rule without attributing it to a tradition we have not adequately read: say whether the present claim rests on direct inspection, somebody else's report, a calculation, a declared assumption, or an invention. These statuses identify the route by which the claim arrived. They do not rank every direct experience above every report. An instrument can deceive; a trusted report can be better than a hurried observation.

This rule also fails if it turns into a stamp applied once. A source may be inspected and misunderstood. A calculation may be exact under a bad assumption. A confident report of an absence may record only an unsuccessful search.

*Disputation:* The visitor says that the index is a machine. The teacher asks what that word permits the visitor to ignore. The visitor answers that it permits counting. The teacher asks why the visitor needed to ignore anything to count.

This workshop composed the visitor and teacher’s exchange as fiction.

## Oration at the vacant centre

<!-- leaf: oration -->

The path reaches the place. Nothing has thereby been said.

The path persists. Nothing has thereby been remembered in words.

The path branches. Nothing has thereby been asked.

We have watched the lines approach one another and have called the meeting an answer. Let us now name the question, the material, and the rule by which a meeting would answer it.

If the line was drawn to join the stations, let it not astonish us by joining them. If it was rewarded for becoming short, let its brevity give no testimony about justice. If we have removed every traveller who failed to arrive, let us put their departures back into the record.

There may be work here which no single traveller planned. There may be a design here which every traveller was made to obey. The finished drawing cannot always tell us which. Give us the making as well as the impression.

We ask for a field that can disappoint its maker. We ask for the unused rule and the unshared trace to be admitted beside the successful image. If the images are equally fine, let the result remain equal.

Do not seat a sovereign in the vacant centre merely because the paths surround it. Do not declare the centre free of power merely because its chair is empty.

## Reading leaf — the work still owed by the fields

<!-- leaf: text -->

The fields perform four different operations: deposit a trace, sense a local portion of it, follow a trace, and allow it to decay. A fifth is a network relaxation between fixed nodes. The five notae in this edition are outputs of those operations under one declared rule (an agent model after Jones 2010, in `fields/physarum.py`), each with a run manifest whose identifier the plate carries in its frame; a validator regenerates every field from its manifest. That is reproducibility, not evidence. [S9]

The production assignment of Follow to Tarot, Decay to the Yijing, and Deposit and Sense to Ifá is an artistic juxtaposition. It does not show that the three traditions instantiate those mechanisms. The particular pairings can be changed without rewriting their histories.

Before a field supports a claim, its maker owes the rule, initial state, seed, boundary conditions, update order, stopping time, and the version of the implementation. A seed and five parameter names cannot recover an image if these choices remain unstated. A manifest identifier is useful only while its target remains available.

The comparison also needs a declared task. For that task, retain a run without shared traces, a run without decay, and a run whose relevant trace access differs. Report failures and equal performance. A beautiful field is an illustration until the comparison does evidentiary work.

A hierarchy can use shared traces. A leaderless group can hide its traces. Local perception need not bound collective extent when information is relayed. Persistent marks can preserve useful memory as well as interference. The hypotheses inherited as D11–D14 must survive these cases or narrow themselves around them.

**DOES NOT CLAIM:** that stigmergy guarantees emancipation, that trails are texts, or that an observer who can read a signal has permission to act on it.

*Another hand:* No empirical claim is discharged by the fields surrounding this paragraph. A field that regenerates identically has shown only that it was computed.

## Condemnation by the seven offices

<!-- leaf: text -->

The following proceedings belong to the fiction of Book III. They are not a medieval document or a paraphrase of Aquinas.

The First Office, having six hands, found that the leaf had entered no hand in the prescribed order. It required the answer to be returned to the beginning. The beginning was not identified.

The Second Office, having two faces, found both that the answer lacked an author and that the author had concealed the answer. It retained both findings pending the appointment of a third face.

The Third Office held a roof over the leaf and required proof that nothing outside the roof had contributed to its contents. The roof had been made elsewhere. This circumstance was ruled immaterial.

The Fourth Office spoke through three mouths. The first required a single account. The second required an independent account. The third found the existence of two accounts to be an inconsistency in the applicant.

The Fifth Office sent the forepart of a horse to inspect the route. It returned a finding of non-arrival. The destination appealed; the Office had no means of receiving an appeal from a place.

The Sixth Office lowered a curtain of bars. Everything visible between them was declared the complete field. Everything behind them was entered as having failed to appear.

The Seventh Office sat in a high chair with no front. It commanded the bearer to approach from the authorized direction.

The leaf was condemned for returning an answer without a ticket, for citing a corpus held by no office, and for leaving a blank which had not been reserved by authority.

The order was unanimous. The minutes record seven different grounds.

## Objection left with the condemned leaf

<!-- leaf: text -->

A reader has written below the sentence: The offices are absurd; therefore the leaf is sound.

Another hand has crossed out therefore.

An office may be arbitrary and its critic mistaken. A source may be excluded for bad reasons and contain a false statement. The office's failure does not confer a gift of truth upon whatever passed outside it.

The historical objection associated with this book's borrowed form is more demanding than the seven offices' complaint. In Summa theologiae II–II, question 96, article 1, Aquinas disputes whether gazing at figures and using prescribed words can produce knowledge by the means claimed. His theological explanation is his own; this workshop takes no verdict on spirits from it. The question about the adequacy of a proposed means remains relevant to our manufactured figures. [S8]

Can an arrangement help someone notice a relation? The present draft proposes exercises to try. Can it establish an external fact merely by being read? No such result is supplied here. Can attention be disciplined without being deceived? That is work for the reader and an appropriate comparison, not an accomplishment conferred by the title Ars.

The leaf remains beside its condemnation. The two do not cancel. Neither constitutes the evidence missing from the other.

*In the gutter:* A prohibition is not a refutation. A prohibition is not a certificate.

## Three appointments of the Keeper

<!-- leaf: text -->

**Before the shuffle.** Record the question, the positional convention, and one fact that would make a proposed reading untenable. After the reading, distinguish an observation newly sought because of the cards from an observation already known. A prompt can lead to a discovery without having predicted it.

**Between the line and its explanation.** Preserve the cast before selecting passages or drawing a second arrangement. Record the method and the rules used for changing lines. If two commentators give different instructions, let their instructions remain attributable to them. The new result is not evidence that the former cast never occurred.

**Before erasure.** Decide what must be retained for the claim under examination, and what the observer has no entitlement to retain. This is an instruction for this workshop's own experiments and records. It does not prescribe the documentation of somebody else's ritual or authorize copying a restricted account.

The three appointments are optional exercises for reading the present artwork. They are not divinatory instructions recovered from its three subjects. No result from them is reported as having been obtained.

There is a fourth appointment, made only if required: after the comparison fails. Keep that leaf as well. A method which yields no useful difference on its stated task has supplied a result. It has not secretly succeeded by teaching humility.

*Another hand:* The Keeper may be dismissed. A reader who already does this work needs no additional office to oversee it.

## Register of propositions

<!-- leaf: text -->

**N1. Correspondence and descent.** An exact mapping of chosen features is insufficient, by itself, to establish a historical route or a maker's intention. This is an evidentiary distinction, not a claim that all correspondences were imposed. A particular genealogy should be assessed from its witnesses. The brief's universal retrojection thesis is withdrawn.

**N2. What an order does.** For any procedure under discussion, specify whether order controls rank, retrieval, comparison, interpretation, or merely presentation. A controlled reordering that leaves the chosen operation unchanged counts against a claim that this order is essential to that operation. No ranking of the three traditions by mathematical sophistication follows.

**N3. The material analogy.** Inscription and erasure can place otherwise different practices beside one another in an artwork. A shared mechanism would require evidence of a shared causal operation. The draft makes no such finding. The analogy may remain aesthetically useful even when a mechanistic hypothesis fails.

**N4. The route of a claim.** Recording inspection, report, calculation, assumption, and invention may expose errors concealed by an undifferentiated authoritative voice. This is this workshop's proposal. Compare reviews with and without these distinctions; if they reveal no additional consequential errors, the claimed practical benefit has not been shown. Its value as literary form is a different question.

**D11–D14. Fields.** Shared traces, persistence, sensing and action permissions remain model-specific questions. The relevant null results and counterexamples are stated on the field's reading leaf. No supporting run has been made for this edition.

These are different kinds of statement. Historical claims need witnesses; calculations need checked premises and operations; empirical proposals need comparisons; artistic decisions can be revised without pretending to have been falsified.
