# The bounded argument

## A broken invariance

Let logical progress be `s = g(t) = log(1+t)`. The function is increasing and
onto the nonnegative logical times. Reaching any finite logical time `L` still
takes a finite physical time, `exp(L)-1`. But a physical deadline `H` permits
only `log(1+H)` progress. An eventual reachability event need not remain a
deadline-qualified reachability event after slowing.

This is a consequence of the stated clock. It neither refutes the fluid
construction nor supplies a universal biological or political law. In
particular, it requires no loss of stored information. An intact route can
become too late.

## Dependencies make the next step exact

Define an option as usable for a given modeled task when all its required
prerequisites are available and a feasible schedule meets its deadlines and
declared resource constraints. An archive entry is insufficient evidence of
that condition.

`generator/reachability.py` handles one narrow case: finite acyclic AND
prerequisites, known nonnegative durations, release times, task completion
deadlines, unrestricted parallel capacity, and one total cost budget. It
recursively takes the maximum prerequisite completion, not their sum, then
adds the next duration. Shared ancestors are charged once. Tests include a
counterexample to treating parallel durations as serial and to counting a
late-opening route as available in time.

The proof is elementary induction over a topological order. The assumptions,
not the mathematical accuracy, are the vulnerable bridge to an actual case.
Task deadlines concern that task's completion only. They do not model a stored
output expiring while a later task waits to use it.

## Proposed institutional insight

If future options require maintained capacities, then maintenance decisions
can alter the future feasible set without changing a formal decision rule.
Keeping one reader, skill, relation, or procedure usable while letting another
lapse may select among alternatives before a vote. This is **selection by
maintenance**, a proposed organizing question, not a priority claim or a
validated empirical law. Motive is not inferred from the dependency alone.

A further question follows within the author's values: does an affected person
retain a usable route to contest that maintenance policy before the capacity
lapses? This is a constitutional demand adopted by the project. Scheduling
does not derive it. A system can be feasible and unjust.

## What the bibliography adds

- Ashby supplies the question of available regulatory responses and channels;
  he already treats temporal constraints. This edition claims no discovery of
  an omission in his work.
- Boussard et al. supply one bounded dormancy/memory result. Kramar and Alim
  supply a different material-memory mechanism. Neither supplies politics.
- Taylor's abeyance account makes continuity, including its centralization and
  exclusions, historically examinable. It prevents romanticizing preservation.
- Ulrich and Reynolds ask who is affected without being involved. The proposed
  temporal extension asks whose later options today's maintenance excludes.
- Ostrom encourages examination of actual rules and interacting centers;
  Freeman distinguishes attributed public representation from chosen authority.

These contributions remain different. The book does not treat their
agreement with one image as independent confirmation of a general theory.

## How the proposal could fail

Ordinary review and scheduling may already find the same mechanisms at lower
cost. Maintenance may follow merits rather than shape the relevant choice.
Unmaintained alternatives may remain usable, or new alternatives may appear.
Deadlines may be misdescribed. Recovery coding may have poor agreement. The
book has no solution for an institution that is itself the hazard and offers
no effective corrective recipient.

The result should then be limited or abandoned as a practical method. The
artwork does not need the method to become a theory of everything.
