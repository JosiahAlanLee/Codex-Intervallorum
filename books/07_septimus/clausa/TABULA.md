# Nota Clausa

## Tabula

Seven fields follow, grown by the rule of the notae. Each carries a reading that the growth has scattered: every mark is an agent of the mould, red for one and black for nought, six to a sign. The rule is printed whole in the Regula. The key is not printed.

The key is three names, written in capitals with one space between them, in this order. The books bound here hold all three.

The first is the spirit of the third book who permits a diagnosis only when no doubt can remain about it.

The second is the woman, in the parable of the mothers of the night, whose threshold a lilith keeps.

The third is the village that gives its name to the proceeding of 1519 in the sixth book, in which villagers seized an accused woman and held her without authority.

Here the names work, and for no reason a spirit would recognise. Each is a cause in the computation and a sign to no one. Speak them wrongly and the mould grows another way.

What the seven fields carry are objections, in seven articles and against a colophon, to the Quaestio printed open at the end of De Vinculis. That disputation was the first text sealed here. Its marks lay in the order of the agents, and it could be read without the key. These marks lie in the order of the ground. The objections cite only works already in the books' registers. They give no instructions. Their digests are printed below so that a reading can be checked without trusting anyone who claims it.
