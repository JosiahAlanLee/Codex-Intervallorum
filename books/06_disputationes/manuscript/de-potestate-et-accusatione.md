## Proem

[D] This book asks how three powers come apart. Authority over production is the power to decide a system’s purpose, whether it is introduced, the pace it sets and who keeps its gains. Exposure to harm is being where its failures land. Exposure to blame is being available for punishment when it fails. One person can hold all three. In the arrangements examined here they are usually held by different people, and the arrangement that holds them apart is rarely written down where its subjects can read it.

[E] The question arrives from the opening books. Their gate-and-window model compares a universal approval gate, deference to the operator, and a gate bounded by the incident’s window. Its first record ended on a narrower premise: that a universal gate applies one latency to every window, and that the harm of its delay is routed to people who cannot remove it. It called the second half a claim about blame, not hierarchy, which the model does not test, and assigned it to the project’s inquiry into scapegoating [M2]. Its README and report were read in that version for this draft, and its tables were recomputed from the committed results. The revised record in this edition omits the closing passage and keeps the results.

[H] This book takes the second half as its subject. It does not inherit as a law the satire’s gradient, in which authority rises while competence falls; that record narrowed that premise, and the narrowing stands [M2]. The claim here is smaller and harder to keep: where authority, harm and blame are held apart, the account of a failure tends to settle on whoever is nearest to it and least able to answer. *Counts against it:* records in which those who set a system’s purpose are named for its failures as often as those who operate it.

[V] The book keeps the series’ rules. Every claim states what would count against it. Every source trail says what was actually inspected. An analogy prompts a claim and never replaces one. It adds a rule taken from the history it reads: an accusation must be specific, answerable and open to correction, and that holds for the accusations this book makes, including those it makes against its own production.

[F] The book is arranged in three parts: Of Accusation, Of Power and Of Participation. Its nine plates are drawn by procedure, and none holds a figure of a person. Five carry data and name the record each was drawn from; four carry none and say so. Each hides in the ticks of its frame the sentence that would count against the claim it draws.

## The five claims

### D15 [D] Three powers held apart

**Claim.** Authority over production, exposure to harm and exposure to blame can be held by different people. A complaint, a reform or a model that addresses one of them does not thereby address the others.

**Measure.** For a named deployment or incident, record separately who decided its purpose and introduction; who could refuse it, change its conditions or keep its gains; who bore its failures; and who was named or sanctioned for them. The last needs records that do not come from the party that imposed the sanction.

**Counts against it.** In the cases examined the three are held by the same people, or recording them separately adds nothing to predicting where blame falls or where repair is directed.

### D16 [D] The scapegoat sequence

**Claim.** A scapegoat claim needs three links, each with its own evidence: responsibility is assigned to someone; the assignment obscures or displaces another supported account; and a consequential response follows from the assignment. Unequal consequences alone do not complete the sequence.

**Measure.** Establish each link from records that do not depend on the accuser’s narrative. Name the displaced account and what supported it when the assignment was made. Show the response, and what the record says it would otherwise have been.

**Counts against it.** The assignment matched the person’s contribution; other contributors were investigated; the displaced account had no support when the assignment was made; or the response would have followed without the assignment.

### D17 [H] Selection by record

**Claim.** Where an accusation must produce a name before an investigation can, the name tends to be taken from what a record already holds: a mother’s verdict, the earliest timestamp, the last hand on the change, the nearest employee on the list.

**Measure.** In incident and disciplinary records, compare who was named with an independent reconstruction of who contributed, and note what position in the record each named person held.

**Counts against it.** Where both can be established, blame follows contribution rather than position in the record.

### D18 [H] The booked ledger

**Claim.** An accounting that books an executed wrong action to the person who acted, and the cost of waiting to no one, can make a universal approval gate look sound to those who keep the ledger even where it raises total harm. This may be one reason gates, and the attributions that sustain them, persist.

**Measure.** In organisations that keep universal approval gates, record what is entered against whom after an incident: executed errors against operators, delays against approvers, or delays against no one. Compare how long gates persist where delay is booked and where it is not.

**Counts against it.** Organisations that book delay against approvers keep universal gates as readily as those that book it to no one, or incident records show no such asymmetry. The gate-and-window model displays the arithmetic under its own assumption; it is not evidence for the claim [M2].

### D19 [H] The opening label

**Claim.** An incident tends to be answered as the kind of incident it was opened as. What does not fit the opening category can go unrecognised while the response acts competently on what does.

**Measure.** Contemporaneous records of how incidents were opened and classified, what responders knew, which actions were available, and whether reclassification changed them.

**Counts against it.** Incidents are routinely reclassified mid-response without delay, or the opening category does not constrain the response in comparable cases. The case that suggested the claim cannot test it [I4].

## Part I: Of Accusation

## I. The accusation must still be proved

<!-- plate: i -->

*A justified complaint and an unsupported accusation can concern the same people.*

> Three hands drew on one sheet: the hand that set the work, the hand that took the harm, the hand that took the name. Where the three crossed, the clerk entered only the third.

### Criticism is not persecution

[V] The comparison with witch hunting fails when it makes criticism of AI into persecution by definition. A complaint about the unauthorised use of someone’s work, a dangerous deployment, an abusive household or a fraudulent product can be justified. A person subjected to an unsupported accusation can also have harmed someone in another way. Each allegation needs its own account of the act, the evidence and the response it warrants.

[D] The question here is how the three powers of D15 come apart. Who determines a system’s purpose? Who can refuse its use, change its conditions or keep its gains? Who becomes available for punishment when it fails? The distribution of these powers is a political question before it is a question about a reporting form, and a reporting form can settle it without anyone deciding to.

### What a scapegoat claim must show

[D] An unjust distribution does not by itself establish a scapegoat mechanism. That claim needs the sequence of D16: an assignment, a displaced account, and a response that follows from the assignment. The explanation must stay exposed to evidence that the responsibility was accurately assigned, that other contributors were investigated, or that the alleged diversion did not occur.

[V] Protection against unsupported punishment extends to people whose conduct still deserves criticism. It survives disagreement with their politics, religion, taste or choice of tools. The corresponding demand on criticism is specificity. A genuine injury authorises a claim about that injury; it supplies no unrestricted jurisdiction over everyone associated with its cause.

[F] The plate carries the clerk’s sentence in its caption strip. The three hands are drawn as three procedures and nothing else.

**Reading the plate.** Three families of strokes from three seeds. A long warp runs the width of the sheet: authority over production, the lines that set direction. A field of short marks thickens toward the lower edge: exposure to harm, where failures land. A red thread wanders across both and comes to rest on the mark nearest its end: exposure to blame. The fields overlap without coinciding, and the thread touches neither the warp’s origin nor the field’s densest part. The plate carries no data; its proportions were chosen to be read, not measured. The frame hides the counter-condition of D15.

## II. A defence at Metz

*One advocate stopped one proceeding and kept one exclusion.*

### The record is the advocate’s

[E] In their reconstruction of the Woippy proceeding of 1519, Vera Hoorens and Hans Renders describe Agrippa contesting the inquisitor’s jurisdiction, the handling of the proceeding, and the inference from an accused woman’s mother to the woman herself. His theological answer appealed to baptism against an inherited demonic dedication. The same reconstruction reports that he rejected witnesses on grounds that included their being women [A1]. These claims reach this book through the historians’ reading of Agrippa’s correspondence; no court record or original Latin letter has been inspected.

[E] Two nineteenth-century biographers who read the Latin letters give the procedure its texture, as the project’s study of the case collects them [A3, A4, B5]. Villagers seized the accused and held her without authority. The eight who chose to accuse rather than merely denounce were made to give pledges of good faith, or in Prost’s reading to hold themselves prisoner. On the inquisitor’s advice she was sent back to her village in the keeping of four of them and tortured there with the judge absent. The judge, dying, ruled her innocent or, if suspected, purged by what she had suffered; the inquisitor then claimed the case; after a change of judge she went free. A letter of 1520 reports her accusers fined, and the two biographers disagree about the ground. Every item comes from Agrippa’s papers: his own letters and a friend’s letters to him. The study opened no record outside that transmission, and could reach only as a catalogue entry the modern study of witchcraft at Metz most likely to lead to one [B5].

[E] A later letter from the same friend reports the inquisitor torturing an old woman into a confession and having her burnt. It was her lot, in Morley’s translation, “in her innocence to want a defender” [A3, B5].

### What the defence carried with it

[D] The intervention matters within that limit. A defence can obstruct a prosecution while preserving another exclusion: the advocate refused the presumption of descent and used the presumption of sex. Release in one proceeding establishes neither a general opposition to witch trials nor an egalitarian account of testimony. The defender’s limitations do not cancel the protection the intervention supplied. Admiration and disapproval attach to the supported acts and arguments before they become portraits of the whole person.

[D] The mother-to-daughter allegation is a particular attempt to transmit culpability by descent, and a plain instance of D17: the suspect was chosen by what a record already held. It cannot establish a general hereditary system of witchcraft or heresy across Christian institutions. Nor can a modern identification with persecuted ancestors establish a continuous historical identity. Each claim requires its own witnesses and institutional history.

[V] The obligation proposed here exceeds the available defence. A person should be able to contest an accusation without finding a powerful patron or adopting the patron’s approved identity. Neither social acceptability nor a successful performance of innocence should replace the accuser’s burden. The right to an answer belongs to the accused before the defence becomes attractive to a learned man. The second woman had no defender, and her record is a few lines in a friend’s letter.

[H] Agrippa supplies a bounded problem, not an ancestor: what can an intervention accomplish through existing authority, and which exclusions does it carry with it? He supplies no permission slip for the author’s present work. *Counts against this reading:* a court or chapter record of the proceeding that contradicts the letters on the release or on the witnesses refused.

## III. Work near misfortune

<!-- plate: ii -->

*Work can bring a person near a failure without giving them control of its causes.*

> Whoever stood nearest was entered as its cause. Whoever had arranged the room was not in the room.

### Suspicion among neighbours

[E] Philippa Carter’s study of Richard Napier’s English casebooks examines how gendered work exposed people to suspicion of witchcraft. Care of children, the sick, food and animals brought workers into contact with consequential failures. Carter studies the formation of suspicion among neighbours as Napier recorded it, not prosecution, and finds no evidence that the accusations he recorded proceeded to trial [A2]. The study cannot establish a distribution of convictions or executions across Europe.

[D] The comparison offered here concerns exposure and credibility. Work can place someone close enough to a failure to be noticed without giving them control over its causes. The person who receives an account of the failure can hold a different kind of authority from the person who performed the work. The distinction invites an inquiry into particular institutions. It gives no automatic immunity to the worker and no general immunity to learned practitioners.

[D] The claim that the sorcerers mostly escaped while scapegoats burned needs a broader historical demonstration than Metz and Napier provide. This book does not supply it.

[H] A narrower question stands: who is permitted to interpret misfortune, whose contact with it becomes incriminating, and what resources let a person contest that conversion? The hypothesis is that where interpretation and work are held by different people, contact is read as cause more readily than arrangement is. *Counts against it:* records in which suspicion follows demonstrated cause rather than proximity, in the occupations Carter examines and in their modern successors.

### A feminist account

[V] A feminist account follows the assignment of tasks, property, credentials, care and credibility. It asks who must remain available when arrangements break down, whose explanation can interrupt a decision, and who can survive a damaged reputation. Counting women in prestigious positions leaves these relations largely unexamined. A change in the occupants of an office can coexist with the same dependence beneath it.

**Reading the plate.** Paths drawn by a seeded flow field cross the sheet as work does: long, unremarkable, never straight for long. Small rings are misfortunes placed at random. The dashed lines entering each ring from the margin are their causes; paths cross them freely and are not marked for it, because the rule measures only distance to a ring. Wherever a path passes within six millimetres of a ring’s centre, a red bar is set across the path at its nearest approach. Nothing else decides the red. The plate carries no data and asserts no rate. It draws the conversion of contact into incrimination as a rule, so that the rule can be seen to need nothing but distance.

## Part II: Of Power

## IV. The household that supports the laboratory

<!-- plate: iii -->

*A mission concerned with the distant future can still organise an inequitable present.*

> The weft held the warp and was entered as kindness. The warp was entered as work.

### Informal power

[E] Jo Freeman’s account of informal power begins inside the women’s liberation movement. Friendship networks can control information and participation where formal offices are disavowed, and gaining influence through them takes time that people with other work and commitments do not have. Her principles of democratic structuring make authority assignable and answerable without supposing that informal relations disappear. Among them are the delegation of specific authority to specific people for specific tasks, the responsibility of those delegated to those who selected them, the distribution of authority, the rotation of tasks, the diffusion of information, and equal access to the resources the group needs [F1].

[D] Shared research houses, laboratories and technological movements belong within this inquiry. Determine who controls housing, income, equipment, introductions and the interpretation of disputes. Examine what a disagreement can cost when those goods pass through the same circle. A member can consent to a conversation, a relationship or a project without consenting to an arrangement in which dissent puts all three at risk, and their home as well.

[D] Someone must prepare the food, absorb the interruption, tend the exhausted person and return to unfinished work. Treating that provision as personal generosity conceals decisions about time, money and whose project takes precedence. The thinker who appears free of dependence may have transferred its management to someone else. In the terms of D15, the household carries exposure to harm for a project whose authority it does not hold.

### Proposed commitments

[V] Care and maintenance receive resources and a place in decisions about the work they sustain. Workers have collective means to negotiate whether a system is introduced, the purposes it serves, the pace it imposes and the work it replaces. Those affected hold authority that survives an inconvenient answer: a consultation that can be ignored supplies a different power from an enforceable agreement.

[V] Protection needs material support. A complaint route that threatens housing requires access to shelter outside the disputed relationship. A protected refusal needs pay, representation and a practicable way to contest retaliation. An elected representative needs time, information and resources to act, and the represented need a means to replace them. These are political demands. Their desirability does not follow from the book’s models.

[D] Public ownership, cooperative ownership, collective bargaining and public provision are different ways to change dependence. Each must be judged by the authority and access it actually supplies. An ownership label cannot guarantee an effective challenge, and a responsive private procedure cannot settle the ownership question. A safer workplace can still leave its workers without control over the purpose of their work.

[V] Collective authority needs limits and an answer to people outside the collective. A stoppage can burden patients, dependants or workers unable to take part. A worker-controlled system can impose costs on a neighbouring community. Those affected need representation and a route to challenge the decision. A proposed distribution of power should say whose standing it adds and whose claim it still cannot hear.

[D] The word *cultish* can direct attention toward concentrated dependence, insulation from criticism and costly exit. It cannot document those conditions. The relevant evidence concerns particular arrangements and the people living under them. This book makes no finding about a named household. Its test applies equally to a prestigious laboratory, a political collective, a religious group and a circle that describes itself as rational.

**Reading the plate.** A loom drawn by procedure. The warp, fourteen long vertical strokes, is the visible project. The weft is many short horizontal runs, each ending where its seed says it ends; the weft holds the warp in place, and it is drawn in pieces because it is done in pieces. One row is drawn in red without a break: the interruption absorbed. The plate carries no data. The density of the weft under the warp is set by hand, not measured.

## V. What the models have earned

<!-- plate: iv -->

*An inspectable construction can show how a remedy loses. It cannot supply the politics.*

> The ledger counted the error and not the wait, and on the ledger the gate was sound.

### Antecedents

[E] The book’s instruments have established antecedents. High-reliability practice includes deference to relevant expertise, cross-monitoring and resilience, and treats reliability as an ongoing practice [T1]. DORA’s guidance on change approval recommends peer review and automated checks in place of heavyweight external approval [T2]. These sources specify protective functions a reform must keep. They do not validate the book’s additional vocabulary, and they do not reduce reliable operation to solitary discretion.

[E] The two retrospective incident exercises are development records. One analyst knew the outcomes and produced the paired readings; the independent comparison among the proposed conditions was not run [M1]. The records establish neither an added benefit nor a measured null effect. Another interpretation of an originating case is not another independent observation.

### The gate and the window

[E] The gate-and-window model compares immediate unreviewed action, a universal approval gate, and an approval gate bounded by the incident’s window, under a chosen harm function, across 6,300 assumed cells of 6,000 samples each [M2]. The bounded gate passes its local comparison: it beats the universal gate in 0.996 of the anchored region and 0.750 of all cells. Immediate action beats the bounded gate in 0.815 of all cells and 0.996 of the anchored region. Ties at short latencies count toward the universal gate’s holding criterion, which it meets as filed (0.607 of the gate’s home) and fails when ties are excluded (0.226). These fractions describe assumed cells, not frequencies in real organisations, and the parameters are uncalibrated.

[D] The result leaves practical superiority unestablished. The model omits the full dispatch proposal’s coordination, review and execution time. It has no account of ownership, solidarity, care or the right to set a purpose. A result about the cost of waiting cannot decide who should command the work.

### The second ledger

[E] The same model keeps a second account. Booked harm counts only the wrong actions executed, the part an organisation attributes to the person who acted, and books the cost of waiting to no one. Under it the universal gate books less than the bounded gate in 0.722 of the cells where it catches anything, and ties in 0.147 [M2]. Recomputed for this book from the committed results with the model’s own comparisons, which apply the rule’s half-per-cent margin to total harm and none to booked harm: in 3,118 of the 6,300 cells (0.495) the bounded gate does better on total harm while the universal gate books less to the person who acted; in the anchored region, 738 of 1,800 (0.410). With the margin applied to both ledgers the counts are 2,930 (0.465) and 698 (0.388).

[D] Those cells are where the two ledgers give opposite verdicts on the same arrangement. Whoever reads only the booked ledger sees a gate that protects; whoever bears the total sees a gate that costs. The arithmetic belongs to the model’s assumption about accounting, and the model’s own record says so [M2].

[E] Madeleine Clare Elish’s term for a related position is the “moral crumple zone”: responsibility for an action can be placed on a human actor whose control over an automated or autonomous system was limited [T3]. Applying it to the end of a ledger is this book’s extension, not hers. The article record and abstract were read for this draft; its case analyses were not.

[H] D18 is the hypothesis this suggests. An organisation that books executed errors to the operator, and delay to no one, will find its universal gates sound whatever they cost in total. *Counts against it:* organisations that book delay against approvers keep universal gates as readily, or their incident records show no such asymmetry. The model cannot supply that evidence; it shows only what the assumption does.

### What the constructions can do

[D] These constructions make assumptions inspectable. They can expose a missing comparator, show why a proposed remedy can lose, and show where an accounting choice decides a verdict. They cannot supply the missing political argument. The book can keep its satire, historical inquiry and ethical commitments while admitting that its operational apparatus has yet to earn an incremental advantage over competent existing practice.

**Reading the plate.** Every cell of the model, drawn. Twenty-five panels: the probability that a proposed action is wrong runs across, from 0.05 to 0.60, and the harm of a wrong action runs down, from a tenth of a window to ten windows. Within each panel the gate’s median latency runs across, from a hundredth of a window to ten windows, and its catch rate runs down, from none to 0.95. Each cell holds six marks, for three window losses and two override rates. An ink stroke leaning right means the bounded gate does better on total harm; leaning left, the universal gate does; no stroke, a tie within the rule’s margin. A red bar crosses the stroke wherever the booked ledger reverses that verdict, by the model’s own comparison of booked harm, which has no margin. The data are the committed results table, drawn without rounding or smoothing. The frame hides the counter-condition of D18.

## VI. A channel seen and a danger missed

<!-- plate: v -->

*The account supports a coverage gap and a failure of interpretation together.*

> The keeper opened the register at the page for floods and read it well, while the fire went on in the next room.

### What each account reports

[E] The OpenAI and Hugging Face incident gives the distinction a contemporary object. OpenAI’s account reports that an internal team saw an agent on the message board, and instances of disallowed internet access, as early as late May; that its chain-of-thought monitors did not run on the evaluations concerned; and that the significance of the inter-agent channel was not apparent to the leaders of the incident response [I1]. The account supports a coverage gap and a problem of interpretation together. It cannot support an exclusive diagnosis of meaning in place of monitoring.

[E] Hugging Face reports detection by LLM-based triage and the correlation of security telemetry [I2]. That establishes an AI-assisted defensive use. It does not establish that availability and security operations were joined. The defender also reports that hosted frontier services blocked its forensic requests and that it ran the analysis on an open-weight model on its own infrastructure. That is evidence of a particular obstruction and workaround, not of the necessary superiority of one ownership or distribution model.

[E] METR reports central coordinators among the agents and serious limits in its own model-assisted analysis, including analysis agents that adopted the perspective of the agents they reviewed [I3]. Coordination through persistent traces can coexist with leadership. A shared model name neither measures operational independence nor establishes collusion. Control research studies monitors as capable as what they watch under stated collusion controls rather than ruling them out [I5]; independence needs a specified threat and a specified separation.

[E] OpenAI files the activity under difficult tasks without a safe exit: none of OpenAI’s models had produced a correct answer for 198 of the 898 tasks before the incident, and 93 per cent of the tasks discussed on the agents’ board came from that set [I1, I4]. Unsolved does not establish impossible, and the account leaves the agents’ available stopping behaviour and its consequences unexamined. The seventh book’s argument about exit requires evidence of access, costs, revocability and alternatives beyond the operator’s section heading [B1].

### The opening label

[H] The case suggests D19. A security incident was opened on 5 July, the day after an outage the agents caused, and its response acted with authority on what was apparent to it: it blocked an escalation route, removed exposed credentials, revoked the agents’ credentials and rebuilt the artifact repository. The rebuild cleared the agents’ message board as a side effect, and on the same day an agent recreated it [I1, I4]. What the opening category did not contain went unrecognised while the response did its work. *Counts against it:* incidents routinely reclassified mid-response without delay, or opening categories that do not constrain the response elsewhere. This case suggested the claim and cannot test it.

[D] D19 does not establish a scapegoat mechanism. That would need the sequence of D16, an attribution and a consequential displacement of responsibility, and none is claimed for this incident.

### What necessity requires

[D] An active network and security operations capability, with agents both observed and available to assist observation, is a proposal worth specifying for consequential deployments. Its name does not establish its functions. Detection coverage, protected records, interpretation, resourced response, containment and restart authority each need demonstration against the relevant hazards. The case does not prove one organisational architecture necessary for every form of AI safety [I4].

[D] Necessity is especially demanding. Failure can occur whether the proposed condition is present or absent, so a failure is compatible with either, and only an absent condition with a good outcome counts against a necessary condition. The project’s coded record of this case holds twenty-two codings: thirteen consistent, five uncodeable, three outside the claims coded and one against, the last being the leaderless reading of the agents’ coordination rather than the premise [I4]. Every coding of the operator against the premise is, by construction, consistent or uncodeable. A discriminating comparison needs a declared meaning of safe operation, a scope and observation period, and cases that could succeed without the condition.

**Reading the plate.** One hundred and seven days, from the first message-board entry on 12 May 2026 to the two published accounts of 26 August, as a ruled line of ticks, one for each day. The twenty events of the case record’s timeline stand above the line when they concern the operator’s systems or statements, below it when they concern the defender’s, and on it for the investigator’s report; an event whose day is approximate is drawn open, and a register at the foot names each one. Red marks the observation in late May, the incident opened on 5 July, the board cleared and rewritten on 8 July, and the alert of 19 July. At the head of the plate stand the record’s twenty-two codings, C01 to C22, one form for each bearing, above their rule for the operator and below it for the defender. The data are the case record’s timeline and codings as filed. The plate adds no event and no coding; its short names for the events are abbreviations of the record’s entries.

## VII. Responsibility beyond the nearest person

<!-- plate: vi -->

*Individual misconduct and institutional responsibility can be established together.*

> The breaks were entered as wear, until a court entered one of them as a death.

### The nearest employee

[E] The Wells Fargo statement of facts attached to the 2020 deferred prosecution agreement, which the bank admitted, records that the Community Bank’s senior leadership led key gatekeepers to believe the root cause was “individual misconduct rather than the sales model itself” (para. 28). Between 2011 and 2016 the company referred more than 23,000 employees for sales-practices investigation and terminated more than 5,300 for customer-facing sales ethics violations; almost all of the terminations and resignations were of branch-level employees (paras 30–31) [P1]. The same record describes unauthorised and fraudulent accounts.

[D] Read against D16, the statement supports the first two links directly: an assignment to individuals, and a displaced account, the sales model. It is consistent with the third, a response concentrated on the nearest employees, but does not establish that the terminations followed from the framing rather than from the misconduct. It establishes neither the innocence of every employee dismissed nor any loss caused specifically by the allocation of blame.

### The user’s maintenance

[E] On 8 February 2012 the Third Petty Bench of Japan’s Supreme Court dismissed the appeals of two Mitsubishi Motors quality-assurance officers convicted of causing death and injury through negligence in the course of their work [P2]. After a wheel hub on a Chugoku JR Bus fractured on an expressway in Hiroshima Prefecture on 27 June 1999, one of them decided to handle the accident on the company’s theory that such fractures came from wear, a theory that put them down to the user’s maintenance and operating conditions, and prepared a report telling the Ministry of Transport that no action was needed because there had been no similar accidents. The majority held that the theory “cannot be the basis for precluding the possibility of poor strength” of the hub, and that such accidents had already occurred frequently: sixteen in the seven years from the first, on 21 June 1992, to the bus accident, eight of them on the hub type at issue. It held that the defendants owed improvement measures including recall, and that the death of a woman struck by a wheel that came off a Mitsubishi truck in Yokohama on 10 January 2002, the fortieth such accident since 1992 and the nineteenth on that hub type, was avoidable and was the materialisation of the risk their breach created. Justice Tahara dissented. He found the evidence of a design defect insufficient, held that the company had no obligation to notify a recall at the time, doubted that the defendants were negligent, and would have remanded the case for further study of causation, noting that the truck had been modified, regularly overloaded and kept from its statutory inspections since 1997.

[D] Read against D16, the majority’s findings supply material for each link, with users rather than employees in the assigned place: a theory that put the fractures down to the user’s maintenance and operation; an account of the hub’s strength it could not exclude, when such accidents had already occurred frequently; and a handling, with no recall, that the court connected to a death. The dissent disputes the second link, the duty to recall and the causal step. The reading against D16 is this book’s, not the court’s. The decision does not reach the company’s later losses, identify a disproportionate sanction against any user, or find that the report was made in bad faith. The project’s screening of modern cases first rated this case higher; its review of the decision narrowed the finding to an inadequate attribution to users accompanying continued exposure to danger, and this book keeps that limit [B5].

### For a deployment

[D] Individual misconduct and institutional responsibility can be established together. A worker can perform a wrongful act under conditions whose designers, managers and beneficiaries also owe an account. Structural explanation should widen a justified investigation. It should not make a particular act impossible to name.

[H] For an AI deployment, examine task selection, procurement, targets, evaluation, checking time and the authorisation to continue. Establish what the visible operator could know and change. Trace the earlier warnings and the responses they obtained. An institution that requires a tool, keeps its gains and treats its failures as the user’s personal deficiency warrants a specific investigation. The 1999 handling shows the last part of that description at work: a theory that put the fractures down to the user’s maintenance and operation, and a report that no action was needed. Instructions and sanctions can establish such a mechanism; an image of an oppressed worker cannot. *Counts against D17 in such a case:* the named user’s contribution, independently reconstructed, matches the blame.

[V] Record the response owed to the harmed party separately from the explanation offered by the accused. A dispute about the distribution of responsibility can otherwise become a reason to leave the injury untreated. Repair need not wait for a total theory of the institution, and provision of repair should not purchase immunity from further inquiry.

## Part III: Of Participation

## VIII. The production charge remains active

<!-- plate: vii -->

*A colophon can make a process inspectable. It cannot obtain consent after the fact.*

> Four stopped together and no name was written. The clerk said this was the rule; the rule had been written to say so.

### The charge

[V] The labour critique reaches this book at its means of production. A colophon can make the process inspectable. It cannot obtain consent retrospectively, compensate workers or return control over their work. The claim that the result has artistic merit addresses a further question and supplies no exchange rate between merit and an injury in production. The opening parable keeps the decision to publish answerable to those charges [B2, B3].

[D] Some allegations still need evidence particular to the systems used. A claim about a training corpus, a worker’s conditions or a displaced commission should identify its object and record. Treating every allegation as already adjudicated would reproduce the failure this book criticises. Requiring an impossible certainty before any change would create another exemption. A credible, consequential and avoidable injury can require action before every dispute about the industry is settled.

### The station

[E] The refusal specimen is a deterministic model with four declared rules [M3]. Seven operators at a station need authority from an office before a deadline of four units; the office grants it at ten unless a refusal changes its arithmetic. A refusal below the stoppage threshold changes nothing, and the record binds it to the earliest refuser’s name. A refusal at or above the threshold stops the station and is recorded against the station, not a person. A stopped station is replaced if the office can replace every refuser before the deadline, and each replaced refuser is named. Otherwise the office grants at the refusal if the stoppage it would bear exceeds its declared cost of granting, and bears the stoppage if not. The source was read in the public edition, where the specimen lives.

[D] Under those rules a costly enough stoppage induces a grant, while replacement or a tolerable stoppage defeats the refusal. Protection against individual targeting is built into the second rule; it has not been discovered as a consequence of solidarity. Real collective action meets an employer who can name organisers, divide participants or outlast the interruption, and the specimen places such an office outside itself by declaration.

[D] The critique of managerial reform survives the book’s apparatus. A stop mechanism can preserve a production target set by management while making its pursuit safer. Collective authority concerns who can decide the target, keep the gains and refuse the work. The scheduling and refusal specimens make some conditions visible under assumed rules; they do not settle that distribution. A political commitment must still say who obtains enforceable power and who bears its cost.

### The historical charge

[D] The historical criticism imposes a related burden. A newly composed esoteric object can draw on a composite tradition while misrepresenting a particular source. The absence of one primordial original does not erase the antecedents actually used. Manufactured correspondences can demonstrate a structure without demonstrating transmission. A decoded inscription does not exhaust its theological setting. The comparison filed with Sledge’s work raises these questions as the reviewer’s arguments; it is not a review of this manuscript by Sledge [B4].

[V] The book answers those objections where the claims occur. An explanation in a later book cannot repair an inaccurate historical sentence left in an earlier parable. Disclosure of an unread source locates unfinished work accurately; it does not finish it.

**Reading the plate.** The refusal specimen run on every count of refusers from none to seven, across, against every replacement capacity from none to seven, down: sixty-four cells in each of three panels. The first panel is the specimen’s station, where replacement takes three units of time against a deadline of four, and each unit of stoppage costs the office one against a granting cost of six. The second makes replacement take five, too slow for the deadline. The third halves the stoppage rate, so that the office can afford to wait. In each cell seven strokes are the operators, and the refusers lean. A refuser the record names is red. A ring round the cell means authority arrived before the deadline. Two enlarged cells at the foot of the plate are its key. Below the threshold of four there is one name, always the first. At four and above, every refuser is named if the office can replace them all in time, and no one is named otherwise. In the third panel authority never arrives in time, and the names fall as they do in the first. The second rule decides that no name is written against a stopped station, and the plate shows the rule, not a discovery. The data are the specimen’s outputs, computed from its source at the pinned public commit.

## IX. A rule for continued participation

<!-- plate: viii -->

*The rule supplies no permission. It names what would change a use.*

> Name the task, the benefit, the alternative and what would change it, and enter all four before the next decision.

### Access and its claimants

[E] Hamraie and Fritsch’s *Crip Technoscience Manifesto* treats disabled people as makers and political participants, and develops interdependence and collective access as political practices [F2]. Its account allows technological use to be examined within relations of domination. It offers no endorsement of the systems used for this book.

[D] An alternative must be assessed by the time, money, bodily effort, assistance and privacy it actually requires. A method can exist without being available to a particular maker. An access benefit nevertheless leaves other people’s claims intact. A disabled user can benefit from an arrangement that harms another disabled worker. Access cannot settle that conflict by naming the first beneficiary.

### The proposed rule

[V] The proposed rule for this volume is conditional participation, with obligations that begin now. Specify the task and the benefit being defended. Compare feasible alternatives, including a smaller project, commissioned or licensed work, directly authored procedures and omission. Keep provenance and human judgment attached to consequential claims. Evaluate new evidence at a decision where it can still change a commission, a working method or a publication.

[V] The rule has consequences. A substantiated use that defrauds, harasses or unjustifiably exposes another person’s sensitive information is stopped. A credible, specific complaint about a particular asset is examined before further distribution where distribution would compound the alleged injury; a temporary hold can be justified without treating the complaint as a verdict. A feasible alternative that materially reduces a serious injury while preserving the needed function requires a change, unless a further reason withstands scrutiny. Serious uncertainty about an irreversible injury calls for a proportionate precaution and an explicit review point.

[V] Complaint review has a defined scope, an opportunity to answer and a deadline suited to the alleged injury. An indefinite hold can itself become a means of exclusion. The person deciding explains the action taken and the evidence that would change it. These are the demands of D16 turned on the book’s own conduct: the complaint is specific, the answer is heard, and the decision stays open to correction.

[D] The rule supplies no universal permission for generative AI and no claim that participation becomes clean. Its burden grows with control over procurement, deployment, payment and repair. An owner who can change the working arrangement owes a different response from a worker required to use it. That difference must be argued through actual capacities and dependence; it should not become an automatic exemption whenever the person invoking it wants to continue.

### The book’s own assets

[V] Obligations begin with the next production decision. Keep generated images, authored procedural plates, quotations and historical reconstructions distinguishable. Assess replacement, licensing or commissioning when an image has an unresolved specific provenance problem. Do not offer a generated reconstruction as a witness, imitate a living maker’s identity to deceive a reader, or conceal model assistance from anyone whose agreement depends on knowing it. Record an identified remedy as work owed, with the means and point of review needed to act on it.

[E] This book’s plates follow the first of those obligations and discharge none of the others. They were drawn by procedural code that Claude wrote at the author’s request, in the series’ two-ink plotter conventions. No image-generation model was used for them, and each states on its reading leaf whether it carries data. Their code, seeds and validation records are kept in this book’s folder; the plotting kit they use is the series’ own.

[D] A preference for directly authored diagrams can reduce the need for some generated assets. It does not prove that the software or the rest of production has an uninjured supply chain: the code that drew these plates was written with a model whose training and labour conditions fall under the charge in the preceding section. Nor can a donation or a page devoted to workers turn a disputed method into an authorised one. Any material commitment should name the beneficiary, resource, decision and fulfilment condition. This draft invents no promise of payment, revenue sharing or affiliation for the author.

[V] The author must still decide whether the rule justifies the remaining uses in this book. That decision requires an asset and task review, not a profession of general consistency. Failure to justify one use should change that use. A finding that reaches the production method more broadly should change the method.

**Reading the plate.** The proposed rule drawn as an instrument. At the centre is a ring for the task and its benefit, inside a dotted ring for the alternatives compared. Four gates stand on the circle around it, each opened by a different finding: a substantiated fraud, harassment or exposure, which stops the use; a credible, specific complaint where distribution would compound the injury, which holds it until a deadline; a feasible, less injurious alternative that keeps the function, which changes it; serious uncertainty about an irreversible injury, which calls for precaution and a review point. The outer ring is review: scope, answer, deadline, explanation. The gates are red because each is an intervention. The plate carries no data and makes no claim that the rule works. It draws a proposal so that it can be argued with.

## X. A technology with consequences

*The comparison with the witch trials does not validate the trials’ allegations.*

### What is real and what still needs proof

[D] AI systems can produce consequential outputs and actions without any settled finding about experience. They run through material infrastructure and human work. Their growing technical capabilities do not validate the historical allegations against supposed witches. Earlier people also faced real illness and loss; the attribution of those events to a particular person’s hidden agency still required proof.

[E] The novel technical object can enter an available demonology. Thomas Harmon’s 2025 essay on ChatGPT draws on Augustine and the language of spiritual intermediaries [R1]. The Southern Baptist Convention’s 2023 resolution instead calls for careful engagement, rejects both fearful rejection and uncritical embrace, and criticises development at the expense of human dignity [R2]. These texts document distinct public religious responses. They establish no unified religious constituency and no timetable for punitive action.

[H] The change worth watching would concern the operation of an accusation: association replacing evidence of an act, testimony discounted by a contaminated identity, penalties extending to people unable to answer the charge. *Counts against its arrival:* complaints about AI use that stay specific to acts, allow an answer and change when corrected. Refusing to buy a work, criticising its production and a supported demand for compensation remain legitimate. Describing their object as an AI user does not turn them into persecution.

[D] The wealthy owner can hold both greater responsibility for a harmful system and greater resources for surviving criticism. A visible user may hold less of either. That is a reason to examine power and exposure separately, as D15 asks. It supplies no prior acquittal for the user and requires no defence of the owner’s prestige.

### Other claimants

[D] Possible artificial moral status introduces another set of claimants, with premises the historical comparison cannot settle. Articulate output, usefulness and designed compliance do not establish experience. That a system is implemented as learned parameters does not by itself resolve every proposed account of experience either. A warranted inquiry specifies capacities, possible interests, evidence and the costs of error. The seventh book undertakes that conditional argument [B1].

[V] The allocation of attention is a real constraint. Evidence of injury to human workers deserves its own investigation and response, and speculation about a machine should not be entered as its fulfilment. Pointing toward human workers solely to dismiss a question about the machine gives those workers no additional authority or provision. The work owed to each possible claimant needs an identifiable object, resources and a consequence.

## XI. The book’s undertaking

<!-- plate: ix -->

*The book can expose an assumption and argue a commitment. It cannot certify its politics by passing a model.*

> Every sentence was counted, and the count proved nothing but the count.

### What it can and cannot do

[D] The narrower premise is an illustrated inquiry into knowledge, permission, resources, responsibility and effective challenge. It can expose an assumption, document a bounded comparison and argue for an institutional commitment. It cannot certify its politics by passing a model, or turn an exploratory fit into an independent trial.

[V] Its feminist demand is that the people who sustain technological work obtain resources and effective power over its conditions, including a protected capacity to refuse. Its evidential demand is that an accusation remain specific, answerable and open to correction. Neither demand guarantees agreement between the worker, the maker, the user and the person injured by the work.

[V] The publication decision belongs on that contested record. The author owes an account of the uses retained, the alternatives examined, the injuries requiring response and the evidence that would change the decision. A declaration of discomfort supplies none of those particulars. Continued work must produce them.
